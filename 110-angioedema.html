Angioedema

Hematology and Oncology · Week 47 · src: Angioedema
Bottom line: Airway first; most angioedema treated the same; hereditary gets specific meds.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Establish airway if concern for progression to obstruction; prepare double airway setup including cricothyroidotomy supplies.
↓
2Give epinephrine IM 1:1,000 mid-anterolateral thigh: adult 0.2-0.5 mg, pediatric 0.01 mg/kg max 0.3 mg.
↓
3Repeat epinephrine every 5-15 minutes up to 3 doses as needed.
↓
4Place on continuous IV epinephrine infusion if impending cardiovascular collapse, failing IM epinephrine, or requiring repeated dosing.
↓
5Remove any possible inciting agents (e.g., bee stinger, ACE inhibitor).
↓
6Treat hypotension/shock from third-spacing and vasoplegia with IV fluids and vasopressors.
↓
7Perform early diagnostic nasopharyngoscopy if any airway involvement signs; anticipate need for immediate airway control.
↓
8Obtain C4 level if hereditary or acquired C1-INH deficiency suspected; serum tryptase for histaminergic angioedema follow-up.
↓
9Use epinephrine, antihistamines, and corticosteroids for histaminergic angioedema.
↓
10For hereditary angioedema, use C1 inhibitor concentrates, ecallantide, or icatibant; evidence limited for ACE inhibitor-induced.

📝 Key Facts

  • Angioedema is sudden, transient swelling of well-demarcated deep dermis, subcutaneous, and submucosal tissues.
  • Most frequently mediated by bradykinin or histamine; distinguishing forms helps because management differs.
  • ACE inhibitor angioedema tends to affect head and neck region.
  • Epinephrine IM: 1:1,000 dilution; adult 0.2-0.5 mg; pediatric 0.01 mg/kg max 0.3 mg.
  • Continuous epinephrine infusion start 0.05 μg/kg/min, increase by 0.05 μg/kg/min every 2-3 min.
  • 18-gauge IV wide open without pressure delivers about 20-30 μg/min (20-30 mL/min); start at 5 μg/min (5 mL/min) and titrate.
  • Bedside epinephrine drip: inject 1 ampule code-cart epinephrine (1:1,000 or 1:10,000, both 1 mg) into 1,000 mL 0.9% NaCl = 1 μg/mL.
  • When securing airway, anticipate difficult airway; consider awake intubation with fiberoptic/video assistance, delay paralytics until vocal cords visualized.

Attending Pearls

  • Airway, airway, airway is the priority; prepare for difficult airway intervention first.
  • Don't get overwhelmed by angioedema pathways; focus on the patient in front of you.
  • All angioedema types are treated the same except hereditary angioedema, which requires specific expensive meds.
  • Hoarse voice with little visible swelling may indicate supraglottic swelling.
  • Epinephrine is the go-to for hypotension because angioedema overlaps with anaphylaxis.

Pitfalls

  • Giving epinephrine IV instead of IM for initial dose.
  • Confusing epinephrine concentrations (1:1,000 vs 1:10,000) leading to 10x dosing errors.
  • Not involving clinical pharmacist or double-checking math for epinephrine drips.
  • Sending patient for radiographic imaging before securing at-risk airway.
