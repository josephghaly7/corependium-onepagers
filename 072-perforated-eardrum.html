Perforated Eardrum

HEENT · Week 28 · src: Perforation of the Tympanic Membrane, Acute Otitis Media
Bottom line: TM perforations usually heal alone; avoid ototoxic drops; use ofloxacin if dirty.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Determine if TM perforation is clean or dirty.
↓
2If clean traumatic perforation: leave alone, no antibiotics, arrange ENT follow-up in 2 weeks.
↓
3If clean perforation, avoid steroid-combination drops; they can interfere with healing.
↓
4If dirty perforation: prescribe topical ofloxacin drops; add oral antibiotics if needed.
↓
5If perforation from acute otitis media: treat with oral antibiotics; consider drops for large perforation.
↓
6If tube otorrhea: use topical antibiotic drops only; no oral antibiotics; teach tragal pump.
↓
7Avoid Cortisporin in any perforation or tube because of ototoxic components.
↓
8If vertigo or suspected ossicular/inner ear injury: consult ENT or follow-up within 24 hours.
↓
9If perforated TM: no swimming; use Vaseline-coated cotton ball for showering.
↓
10If tube: tub/shower okay; use tight earplugs for swimming.

📝 Key Facts

  • 90–95% of traumatic TM perforations heal spontaneously; about 5% don't, especially thermal perforations.
  • Cortisporin contains neomycin and polymyxin B, both ototoxic; avoid if any TM perforation or tube.
  • Topical antibiotic drops achieve local concentrations 10^3 times higher than systemic antibiotics.
  • Most Q-tip injuries only abrade the ear canal because it is angled up 30 degrees and curved.
  • AAO-HNS quality measure: do not treat otitis externa with oral antibiotics.
  • Acute vertigo after trauma may signal perilymphatic fistula at stapes/oval window; needs early ENT.
  • Most acute tympanic membrane perforations heal spontaneously within 4 weeks.
  • Otitis media with effusion resolves spontaneously in 2-3 weeks.

Attending Pearls

  • The ear wants to be left alone; most traumatic perforations heal with watchful waiting.
  • Tragal pump: after drops, push tragus 3–4 times to force medication through tube/perforation.
  • Ofloxacin drops are safe in kids topically and cover Pseudomonas and Staph.
  • Tube otorrhea: topical drops only, no oral antibiotics—easy way to reduce antibiotic use.

Pitfalls

  • Prescribing Cortisporin for any perforated TM or tube—ototoxic ingredients can damage cochlea.
  • Giving oral antibiotics for otitis externa—against AAO-HNS quality measure.
  • Using antibiotic or steroid-combination drops for clean traumatic perforation—unnecessary and may impair healing.
  • Missing need for urgent ENT in vertigo or suspected ossicular/inner ear injury.
