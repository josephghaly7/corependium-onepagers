006

STEMI

Cardiology · Week 3 · src: Acute Coronary Syndromes
Bottom line: STEMI: ECG within 10 min, PCI within 60-90 min; time is muscle, time is mortality.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Obtain a 12-lead ECG within 10 min of ED arrival when ACS is considered.
↓
2Review and document the prehospital 12-lead ECG in every patient with ACS-compatible symptoms.
↓
3If initial ECG is non-diagnostic but symptoms are highly suspicious, repeat ECG every 15-30 min for the first hour at minimum, or use continuous 12-lead ST-segment monitoring.
↓
4Diagnose STEMI using J-point ST elevation relative to the PQ junction in two contiguous leads: V2-V3 >=2 mm in men >=40 y old, >=2.5 mm in men <40 y old, or >=1.5 mm in women regardless of age; other leads >=1 mm.
↓
5Call interventional cardiology for emergency reperfusion for STEMI or STEMI equivalent with ischemic symptoms <12 h after onset, or up to 24 h if there is ongoing ischemia.
↓
6Give aspirin 162-325 mg chewable by mouth as a single dose; we recommend 324-325 mg by mouth as the preferred dose; if rectal aspirin is needed, use 600 mg suppository.
↓
7If aspirin cannot be taken (eg, anaphylaxis), choose cangrelor, clopidogrel, or ticagrelor and discuss alternative antiplatelet agents with cardiology.
↓
8Give anticoagulation per institutional guidelines and/or interventional cardiologist discretion; for STEMI with fibrinolytics give heparin 60 units/kg IV bolus (max 4,000 units), then 12 units/kg/hour IV infusion (max 1,000 units/hour).
↓
9Choose reperfusion: immediate PCI is recommended if ischemic symptoms <12 h after onset, or 12-24 h if there is ongoing ischemia; STEMI diagnosis-to-balloon time should not exceed 60 min at a PCI facility and should not exceed 90 min at another facility; first medical contact-to-balloon time in transfer patients should not exceed 90-120 min.
↓
10Give thrombolytics if STEMI pattern (NOT STEMI), no other contraindications, and first medical contact-to-PCI >90-120 min or anticipated ED arrival-to-PCI >90 min; door-to-needle should be <30 min if possible; transfer immediately to a PCI facility after thrombolytics.

📝 Key Facts

  • For every passing hour that a STEMI patient is not treated properly, mortality increases.
  • Current STEMI ECG criteria: new ST elevation at the J-point relative to the PQ junction in two contiguous leads; V2-V3 >=2 mm in men >=40 y old, >=2.5 mm in men <40 y old, or >=1.5 mm in women regardless of age; other leads >=1 mm.
  • Guidelines recommend emergency reperfusion for STEMI and for NSTEMI with electrical or hemodynamic instability, acutely worsening heart failure, or ongoing ischemia despite maximum medical management.
  • Immediate PCI is recommended if ischemic symptoms are <12 h after onset, or 12-24 h if there is ongoing ischemia.
  • If total time from first medical contact to PCI is estimated to exceed 90-120 min, fibrinolytics are recommended for symptom duration <12 h or 12-24 h if a large area of myocardium is at risk or hemodynamic instability is present.
  • Half-dose thrombolytics followed by immediate transfer for PCI have been described successfully and without an increase in major bleeding, but this protocol was not subjected to a randomized trial.
  • 25% of NSTEMI patients have fully occluded culprit lesions, and NSTEMI patients with occlusion have double the short- and long-term mortality.
  • The traditional STEMI ECG criteria are neither sensitive nor specific for acute coronary occlusion (occlusion MI).

Attending Pearls

  • Time is muscle, time is mortality.
  • Dead meat, don't beat.
  • Once the tissue is dead, it's not coming back.
  • A prehospital 12-lead ECG should be reviewed and documented in every patient with symptoms compatible with ACS.
  • All systems should have prehospital cardiac catheterization lab activation to facilitate optimal door-to-balloon times.

Pitfalls

  • The ECG is the most important initial test; it should ideally be recorded within 10 min of ED arrival when ACS is considered.
  • In patients with ongoing highly suspicious symptoms but a non-diagnostic initial ECG, repeat ECGs every 15-30 min for the first hour at minimum or use continuous 12-lead ST monitoring.
  • The decision to perform immediate reperfusion should only involve troponin when the ECG is not diagnostic and symptoms persist.
  • Troponin elevation must be interpreted in context: among ED patients with at least one elevated troponin, up to 85% have conditions other than type 1 MI.
  • The traditional STEMI ECG criteria are neither sensitive nor specific for acute coronary occlusion.
