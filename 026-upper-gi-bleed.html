Upper GI Bleed

Gastrointestinal · Week 12 · src: CorePendium Lower GI Bleeding, Upper GI Bleeding
Bottom line: Variceal UGIB: target MAP 65, ceftriaxone, transfusion trigger 7, 1:1:1 at 3 units/hour.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assume variceal source in cirrhotic with rapid bleeding until proven otherwise.
↓
2Intubate for airway protection; variceal bleeders all get intubated before endoscopy.
↓
3Place 2 large-bore IVs or rapid introducer catheter; target MAP >65 mmHg.
↓
4Give octreotide 50 mcg IV bolus then 50 mcg/hour infusion.
↓
5Give ceftriaxone 1 g IV or ciprofloxacin 400 mg IV; only mortality-benefit drug.
↓
6Give PPI pantoprazole/esomeprazole 80 mg over 30 min; bolus only, no drip.
↓
7Transfuse to trigger hemoglobin 7; give only 1-2 units if not radically unstable.
↓
8If hanging third unit within one hour, activate massive transfusion 1:1:1 or 6:6:1.
↓
9Add vasopressin 0.03-0.04 units/min; add norepinephrine if skin warm/vasodilated.
↓
10Consider esophageal balloon tamponade if exsanguinating and endoscopy not immediately available.

📝 Key Facts

  • Over 75% of massive GI bleeding cases are from an upper GI source.
  • Transfusion trigger of 7 vs targeting 10: the 10 group had increased mortality.
  • INR in cirrhosis reflects factor VII only; stable patient with INR 1.9 does not need FFP.
  • Antibiotics (ceftriaxone) are the only variceal bleed drug with proven mortality benefit.
  • Jehovah's Witnesses refuse human-derived products: RBC, FFP, platelets, cryoprecipitate, and PCCs (from human plasma); factor VII is synthetic.
  • Blakemore tube: gastric balloon alone is safe-ish for transfer; esophageal balloon is high risk.
  • For Jehovah's Witness, give hypertonic fluid not crystalloid: dilutes temporarily but resolves in ~20 minutes.
  • Minors of Jehovah's Witness parents should still get maximal appropriate management including blood products.

Attending Pearls

  • An upper GI bleed is like a gunshot wound to the belly: you don't know where it's coming from but they'll bleed out and die.
  • Treat variceal bleed like pelvic trauma: if unstable, massive transfusion is almost inevitable.
  • Of all the drugs—PPI, octreotide, erythromycin—the only one with mortality benefit is the ceftriaxone.
  • Cirrhotics are vasopressin- and catecholamine-deficient; add vasopressin/norepinephrine in addition to blood, not instead of it.
  • Football helmet is barbaric; get some flowers in it and brighten up the place.

Pitfalls

  • Over-resuscitating to normal BP: cirrhotics live at lower baseline; systolic 140 means over-resuscitation.
  • Giving FFP for elevated INR in a stable cirrhotic; FFP can make things worse.
  • Giving crystalloid to exsanguinating variceal bleed; dilutes RBCs and clotting factors.
  • Giving a fully balanced 1:1:1 resuscitation to a patient needing only 1-2 units; deleterious, increases morbidity/mortality.
  • Transferring with esophageal balloon inflated; secure gastric balloon only and avoid esophageal balloon.
