026

Upper GI Bleed

Gastrointestinal · Week 12 · 25 min
Bottom line: Variceal bleed resuscitation: target MAP 65, antibiotics, balanced transfusion, consider vasopressin.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1If presumed variceal bleed with shock/hematemesis, start blood products; avoid crystalloid.
↓
2Target MAP 65 or systolic ~90; avoid over-resuscitation to normal BP.
↓
3Give ceftriaxone early; antibiotics are the only mortality-benefit intervention.
↓
4Give PPI 80 mg; no drip needed.
↓
5Give octreotide to lower portal pressure and slow variceal bleeding.
↓
6Transfuse red cells to trigger hemoglobin 7; give only 1-2 units if stable.
↓
7If third unit hung within one hour, activate massive transfusion with 1:1:1.
↓
8Consider TXA but low priority; not high up for GI bleeds.
↓
9Add vasopressin 0.03-0.04 units/min; add norepinephrine if warm/vasodilated.
↓
10If no blood accepted, avoid dilution, give TXA/factor VII, early Blakemore, intubate, transfer for TIPS.

📝 Key Facts

  • INR in cirrhosis reflects factor VII only; stable INR 1.9 does not require FFP.
  • Critical administration threshold: 3 units RBC within 1 hour predicts massive transfusion need.
  • Transfusion trigger 7 vs 10: targeting 10 increased mortality in upper GI bleed.
  • Antibiotics (ceftriaxone) are the only variceal bleed treatment with proven mortality benefit.
  • Jehovah's Witnesses refuse human-derived products: RBC, FFP, platelets, cryoprecipitate, PCCs; factor VII is synthetic.
  • Blakemore tube: gastric balloon alone safer for transfer; esophageal balloon at 45 mmHg is high risk.

Attending Pearls

  • Treat variceal bleed like pelvic trauma: unstable patients likely need massive transfusion.
  • Cirrhotics may be vasopressin- and catecholamine-deficient; add vasopressin/norepinephrine while transfusing.
  • In Jehovah's Witness, place Blakemore early while waiting for endoscopy; every lost unit cannot be replaced.
  • If no TIPS available, consider early transfer after Blakemore rather than waiting for failed endoscopy.

Pitfalls

  • Over-resuscitating to normal BP increases bleeding and mortality; cirrhotics have lower baseline BP.
  • Do not give FFP for elevated INR in stable cirrhotic; INR does not reflect bleeding risk.
  • Avoid crystalloid in exsanguinating variceal bleed; it dilutes hemoglobin and clotting factors.
  • Transferring with esophageal balloon inflated is dangerous; secure gastric balloon and avoid esophageal balloon.
