Rib Fractures + Pulmonary Contusion

Trauma · Week 15 · src: Blunt Thoracic Trauma
Bottom line: No magic rib fracture number; admit based on oxygenation, pain control, age and comorbidities.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess oxygenation and pleural space; if no pneumothorax/hemothorax and no hypoxia, proceed.
↓
2If pain controlled with oral medications and patient otherwise well, discharge home.
↓
3Consider higher rib fractures as marker of force, but do not decide admission by location alone.
↓
4Do not use absolute rib fracture count alone; more ribs imply more force and complications.
↓
5Factor age and comorbidities, especially pulmonary disease, into admission decision.
↓
6If pulmonary contusion present but stable and not hypoxic, contusion alone does not require admission.
↓
7If admitting, choose floor versus ICU based on pain control, age, comorbidities, ventilation needs.
↓
8If ICU needed but unavailable, transfer to facility with ICU capability, not necessarily trauma center.
↓
9Evaluate for rib fixation if flail segment, 3+ displaced fractures, or pulmonary complication; transfer if needed.
↓
10Use multimodal pain control: scheduled acetaminophen or NSAIDs plus opioids as needed.

⏱ Time Targets

Repeat CT for blossoming pulmonary contusion4 to 6 hoursFormer practice of repeat CAT scan in 4 to 6 hours to watch for blossoming; now moved away from due to radiation and cost.

📝 Key Facts

  • No magic number of rib fractures mandates admission; more ribs indicate more force and potential complications.
  • Higher rib fractures imply greater force and possible neurovascular injury, but location alone doesn't decide admission.
  • Age and pulmonary comorbidities increase risk of pulmonary complications and mortality after rib fractures.
  • Pulmonary contusion alone is not an admission indication; 'blossoming' no longer warrants routine repeat CT.
  • Rib fracture displacement classification: nondisplaced = at least 90% overlap; offset = between 0% and 90% overlap; fully displaced = complete bicortical displacement (0% overlap).
  • Rib fixation considered for flail segment, 3+ displaced rib fractures, or pulmonary complication impairing mechanics.
  • Mainstay treatment is supplemental oxygen and multimodal pain control: scheduled acetaminophen/NSAIDs plus opioids as needed.
  • Nerve blocks can be helpful for pain control.

Attending Pearls

  • Focus on the patient, not the CT or X-ray; there is no magic rib fracture number.
  • If oxygenation is adequate and pain is controlled with oral meds, discharge is reasonable.
  • When admitting, ask whether ICU is needed and whether rib fixation evaluation is required.
  • Multimodal pain control is scheduled acetaminophen/NSAIDs plus opioids; epidural has strongest data.
  • If the policy doesn't make sense, sit down with everybody and determine what the right thing is.

Pitfalls

  • Automatically transferring patients with three or more rib fractures without assessing oxygenation, pleural space, and pain control.
  • Using rib fracture number or location alone to decide admission.
  • Admitting or repeating CT for pulmonary contusion solely to watch for 'blossoming.'
  • Assuming all patients with pulmonary contusion need admission; contusion alone is not an indication.
  • Overlooking patient/family transfer burden when transfer provides no clear benefit.
