Chest Pain in Young Patients

Cardiology · Week 7 · 15 min
Bottom line: Chest pain in young adults: different differential, focus on red flags, EKG, selective workup.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Obtain focused history: pain quality, triggers, radiation, exertion, trauma, recent illness, PE risk factors, family history.
↓
2Perform exam: cardiac, lung, skin, extremities, abdomen/flank; assess habitus and reproducible chest wall tenderness.
↓
3Obtain EKG on all young chest pain patients; look for ischemia, arrhythmia, HCM, RV strain, pericarditis.
↓
4Obtain chest x-ray with low threshold; may diagnose pneumothorax, effusion, or other causes.
↓
5Order labs selectively, not shotgun; troponin if concerned for ACS, myocarditis, or cocaine/meth use.
↓
6Risk stratify for PE using history: hormones, travel, immobilization, surgery, trauma, cigarettes.
↓
7If exertional chest pain or syncope, consider HCM, ACS, or arrhythmia and evaluate accordingly.
↓
8If fever with chest pain, consider myocarditis/pericarditis; if shortness of breath, consider PE or pneumothorax.
↓
9If chest pain with drug use, obtain troponin and consider ACS.
↓
10If musculoskeletal or GERD suspected, consider discharge with reassurance and follow-up.

📝 Key Facts

  • Chest pain in young patients is defined as adolescents through young adults, roughly under 40.
  • Every young chest pain patient should get a cardiac exam, lung exam, and EKG.
  • Reproducible pain at a specific costochondral junction is meaningful; diffuse palpation tenderness is not reliable.
  • Red flags: exertional pain, syncope, fever, progressive pain, systemic symptoms, shortness of breath, drug use.
  • ACS in young patients occurs with Kawasaki disease, congenital coronary anomalies, cocaine/meth, familial hyperlipidemia, Marfan.
  • Premature coronary risk factors include nephrotic syndrome/renal failure, long-term cocaine, lupus, long-term steroids, chronic HIV.

Attending Pearls

  • Young patients often have no clear diagnosis; acknowledge frustration and avoid shotgun testing.
  • Good history is painting a picture: know exactly how, when, where the pain started.
  • Don't ask a question you don't want the answer to; avoid unnecessary tests in low-risk patients.
  • Chest pain after cocaine or meth changes disposition and warrants troponin.

Pitfalls

  • Shotgunning labs/troponin in low-risk young patients causes false positives and unnecessary workup.
  • Assuming any reproducible chest pain rules out MI; only specific costochondral junction reproduction is meaningful.
  • Forgetting skin exam; zoster rash can be missed until discharge.
  • Not asking about family history of early sudden death, Marfan, or Kawasaki disease.
