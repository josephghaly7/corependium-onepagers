Sepsis

Infectious Disease · Week 20 · src: Sepsis
Bottom line: Sepsis: life-threatening organ dysfunction from dysregulated infection response; early recognition and treatment reduce mortality.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Maintain high index of suspicion; no effective screening tool rules out sepsis.
↓
2Give balanced crystalloid 20-30 mL/kg IV bolus based on volume status.
↓
3Target MAP >65 mm Hg using fluids and vasopressors.
↓
4Start norepinephrine at 0.01-3 μg/kg/min, titrate every 2-5 min for persistent hypotension.
↓
5Resuscitate before intubation; have vasopressors ready due to RSI and positive pressure instability.
↓
6For shock refractory to fluids and norepinephrine >0.25 μg/kg/min, add vasopressin 0.03 units/min or epinephrine if cardiogenic.
↓
7Consider hydrocortisone 50 mg IV every 6 hours for refractory hypotension.
↓
8Obtain cultures (blood, urine, sputum, CSF, ascites) but do not delay antibiotics >1 hour.
↓
9Give broad-spectrum empiric antibiotics based on local antibiograms; identify and control source.
↓
10For metabolic acidosis pH <7.2, infuse sodium bicarbonate 150 mEq in 1 L D5W at 150-200 mL/hour.
↓
11Check CBC, metabolic panel, lactate, blood gas; use POCUS, X-ray, or CT to identify source.

Vasopressin0.03 units/minute IV infusionAdd for shock refractory to fluids and norepinephrine >0.25 μg/kg/min.
Epinephrine0.01-0.5 μg/kg/minute IV infusionAdd for shock refractory to fluids and norepinephrine >0.25 μg/kg/min with concomitant cardiogenic shock.
Hydrocortisone50 mg IV push every 6 hoursConsider for hypotension refractory to IV fluids and vasopressors.
Sodium bicarbonate150 mEq in 1 L dextrose 5% in water, continuous infusion at 150-200 mL/hour (1.5-2 times fluid maintenance rate)For metabolic acidosis with pH <7.2.

📝 Key Facts

  • Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.
  • Septic shock is sepsis plus persistent hypotension and lactate >2 despite volume resuscitation.
  • MAP target is >65 mm Hg.
  • Initial crystalloid bolus is 20-30 mL/kg.
  • Norepinephrine dose range is 0.01-3 μg/kg/min, titrated every 2-5 minutes.
  • Add vasopressin or epinephrine for shock refractory to fluids and norepinephrine >0.25 μg/kg/min.
  • Hydrocortisone 50 mg IV every 6 hours for hypotension refractory to fluids and vasopressors.
  • Pediatric sepsis: Phoenix Sepsis Score ≥2 identifies potentially life-threatening organ dysfunction.

Attending Pearls

  • Shock is about hypoperfusion, not blood pressure; compensated shock can present with hypertension, tachycardia, and tachypnea.
  • Give a single empiric broad-spectrum antibiotic dose once sepsis crosses your mind; you cannot turn back the clock.
  • Think early source control, not just antibiotics; an obstructed infected stone needs drainage too.
  • Resuscitate before you intubate; septic patients with severe metabolic acidosis are high risk for peri-intubation instability.
  • Treat sepsis with the same urgency as stroke or STEMI; early recognition and resuscitation improve mortality.

Pitfalls

  • Conflating sepsis, shock, and hypotension; missing compensated shock because blood pressure is normal or high.
  • Delaying antibiotics to first identify the source or obtain cultures >1 hour.
  • Automatically giving a fluid bolus before confirming shock is septic, potentially harming cardiogenic shock patients.
  • Failing to address hypercarbia with mechanical ventilation when it causes notable acidemia or depressed mentation.
  • Intubating without volume repletion or vasopressors; RSI and positive pressure can precipitate hemodynamic instability.
