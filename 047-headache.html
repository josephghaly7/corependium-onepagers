Headache

Neurology · Week 18 · src: Approach to Headache
Bottom line: Treat pain first, then screen 12 red flags; most headaches benign, work up bad ones.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Get pre-hospital GCS, BP, stability markers if critical; assess vitals: fever or high BP may signal life-threatening cause.
↓
2Airway: intubate if depressed GCS, aspiration risk, respiratory distress, or rapid mental status decline after resuscitation.
↓
3Avoid succinylcholine in progressive neuromuscular disease or prolonged immobilization; use rocuronium if neuro exam done or not critical.
↓
4Treat pain early by category: NSAIDs/acetaminophen or IV dopamine antagonists; tailor to prior meds and interactions.
↓
5Reassess after a time interval in a dark quiet room; response to simple analgesia is not a diagnostic test.
↓
6Ask about red flags: new onset, hard neuro findings, sudden/maximal onset, fever/immunocompromise, minor trauma, clotting risk, peripartum.
↓
7Also screen progressive daily headache, multiple ill patients/pets, dizziness/ataxia, occult trauma, vision/eye symptoms, neck pain.
↓
8Thunderclap/maximal onset: work up subarachnoid hemorrhage (also dissection if neck pain); do not miss.
↓
9Fever/immunocompromise: give ceftriaxone 2 g IV, consider dexamethasone 10 mg before antibiotics; IV fluids ready in case of pressure drop.
↓
10Elderly temporal tenderness/jaw claudication: prednisone 60-80 mg PO; send ESR (also CRP) and arrange biopsy days later.
↓
11Peripartum (third trimester to ~6 weeks postpartum): think CVT and preeclampsia; obtain MR venogram; preeclampsia can occur even with normal BP.
↓
12Multiple ill patients/pets: check co-oximetry; give 100% oxygen; decide on hyperbaric oxygen.
↓
13Headache plus dizziness/ataxia or any hard neurologic finding: treat as stroke until proven otherwise; do full neuro exam.
↓
14Tumor pattern: progressive morning headaches; if not sick and normal neuro exam, outpatient imaging reasonable; elevate head 30 degrees if mass effect/herniation concern.

📝 Key Facts

  • IV dopamine antagonists abort 80-90% of headaches; IM 60-80%; oral ~40%.
  • Thunderclap headache: maximal within seconds, sometimes 1-2 minutes; with new onset requires SAH workup.
  • Medication overuse is the third most common cause of headache; caffeine, opioids, barbiturates, and OTC preparations can cause withdrawal headache.
  • Giant cell arteritis: ESR usually over 100 but can be 40-50; steroids prevent blindness and do not alter later biopsy.
  • Peripartum up to 6 weeks postpartum: consider CVT and preeclampsia; preeclampsia can present after delivery and without very high BP or looking sick.
  • CO poisoning: pulse ox and blood gas PaO2 are misleading; diagnosis requires co-oximetry; hyperbaric oxygen considered gold standard for high levels or symptoms.
  • Cluster headache special therapy: high-flow oxygen via non-rebreather (FiO2 60-80%) and intranasal lidocaine on affected side.
  • Meningitis now an adult disease; watch prisoners, recruits, college dorms; ceftriaxone 2 g IV, consider dexamethasone 10 mg before antibiotics.

Attending Pearls

  • Treat pain first, then diagnose; most headache patients will be discharged.
  • Any severe recurrent headache can be classified as migraine, and treat with the usual band of therapies; differentiating tension vs migraine is not important.
  • If it looks like a stroke, treat it like a stroke; don't be too smart.
  • Cluster headache patients pace like kidney stone patients; high-flow oxygen works better than anything else.
  • Document red flags as yes/no on chart to show thorough evaluation.
  • Elevate head of bed 30 degrees if brain tumor with swelling/mass effect to prevent herniation.

Pitfalls

  • Do not use response to simple analgesia as a diagnostic test; serious headaches can respond to Tylenol.
  • Do not attribute hard neurologic deficits (weakness, cranial nerve deficit, ataxia) to migraine aura; treat as stroke unless established pattern and improving.
  • Do not assume preeclampsia resolves with delivery; postpartum new headache can be preeclampsia or CVT even with normal BP and looking well.
  • Do not withhold steroids in suspected giant cell arteritis for fear of altering biopsy; biopsy remains positive days later.
  • Do not use succinylcholine in progressive neuromuscular disease or prolonged immobilization (hyperkalemia/lethal dysrhythmia risk).
