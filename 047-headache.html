Headache

Neurology · Week 18 · 36 min
Bottom line: Treat pain early, then screen for red flags; most headaches benign.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Treat pain early with NSAIDs/acetaminophen or IV dopamine antagonist; cluster needs high-flow oxygen.
↓
2Reassess after PO treatment in quiet, dark room; response does not confirm diagnosis.
↓
3Ask about red flags: new onset, neuro findings, sudden/maximal onset, fever, immunocompromise, dizziness.
↓
4If thunderclap or new severe headache, work up subarachnoid hemorrhage.
↓
5If fever/immunocompromise suggests meningitis, give IV ceftriaxone 2 g; consider dexamethasone 10 mg before antibiotics.
↓
6If elderly with temporal tenderness/jaw claudication, start prednisone 60-80 mg and arrange ESR/biopsy.
↓
7If sudden headache with neck pain or minor trauma, obtain CTA/MRA for dissection.
↓
8If peripartum new headache, consider CVT and preeclampsia; obtain MR venogram.
↓
9If multiple household members or pets ill, check co-oximetry and give 100% oxygen.
↓
10If headache with dizziness or hard neurologic findings, treat as stroke until proven otherwise.

💊 Doses

Ceftriaxone2 g IVEmpiric antibiotic for suspected meningitis
Dexamethasone10 mg IVGive before or with antibiotics for suspected acute meningitis
Prednisone60-80 mg POEmpiric treatment for suspected giant cell arteritis

📝 Key Facts

  • IV dopamine antagonists abort 80-90% of headaches; IM 60-80%; oral ~40%.
  • Thunderclap headache maximal within seconds to minutes; new onset requires subarachnoid hemorrhage workup.
  • Medication overuse is third most common headache cause; caffeine, opioids, barbiturates can cause withdrawal.
  • Giant cell arteritis: ESR usually >100; steroids prevent blindness and do not alter later biopsy.
  • Peripartum headache up to 6 weeks postpartum: consider CVT and preeclampsia even with normal BP.
  • CO poisoning: pulse ox and blood gas PO2 are misleading; diagnosis requires co-oximetry.

Attending Pearls

  • Treat pain first, then diagnose; most headache patients will be discharged.
  • Any severe recurrent headache can be treated as migraine; differentiating tension vs migraine is not critical.
  • Cluster headache: high-flow oxygen and intranasal lidocaine are effective; patients often pace.
  • Document red flags as yes/no on chart to show thorough evaluation.

Pitfalls

  • Do not use response to analgesia as a diagnostic test; serious headaches can respond to Tylenol.
  • Do not attribute hard neurologic deficits to migraine aura; treat as stroke unless established pattern and improving.
  • Do not assume preeclampsia resolves with delivery; postpartum new headache can be preeclampsia or CVT even with normal BP.
  • Do not withhold steroids in suspected giant cell arteritis for fear of altering biopsy; biopsy remains positive days later.
