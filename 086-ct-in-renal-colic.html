CT in Renal Colic

Renal and Urogenital · Week 40 · src: Renal Stone Disease
Bottom line: US first in young renal colic; CT for age >75, atypical, or refractory pain.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Obtain urinalysis; check WBCs, RBCs, nitrites, leukocyte esterase.
↓
2Obtain ultrasound first for most patients; no further imaging may be needed.
↓
3Young, uncomplicated stone with adequate pain control ± prior stone: ultrasound may suffice.
↓
4Middle-aged with recurrent stones: ultrasound first; no further imaging may be needed.
↓
5Non-contrast CT if toxic-appearing, febrile, or concern for infected obstructed stone.
↓
6Non-contrast CT if high-risk: renal failure or solitary kidney.
↓
7Non-contrast CT if intractable vomiting/pain or may require urological intervention.
↓
8Consider CT with IV contrast for atypical presentations or alternative/concurrent diagnoses.
↓
9Pregnant first trimester: ultrasound first; MRI if ultrasound equivocal.
↓
10Pregnant 2nd/3rd trimester: ACOG recommends CT; MRI 82% sensitive, 98.5% specific.

📝 Key Facts

  • CT is gold standard; standard-dose sensitivity 96.6%-100%, specificity 95.5%-100%.
  • CT identifies alternative diagnoses in approximately 10.8% of renal colic patients.
  • Ultrasound first yields similar outcomes; 11%-15% with urolithiasis lack hydronephrosis.
  • If hydronephrosis is present, obstructing stone is likely >5 mm.
  • STONE score: male sex, <6 hours pain, non-Black, vomiting, hematuria.
  • Choosing Wisely 2014: avoid CT in patients <50 with history of stones and uncomplicated renal colic.
  • Repeat CT for recurrent renal colic changes diagnosis only 6% of the time.
  • Ultrasound sensitivity 70%-85%, specificity 50%-92% by emergency physicians.

Attending Pearls

  • Ultrasound first approach decreased radiation and CT use without missing dangerous diagnoses.
  • CT remains the gold standard and can identify alternative diagnoses in as many as 10% of cases.
  • Most patients do not need surgery; CT is not required for all.
  • Ultrasound-first led to fewer cumulative CTs and radiation at 3-6 months.
  • STONE score can guide shared decision-making for ultrasound vs low-dose CT.

Pitfalls

  • Using KUB for emergency diagnosis; sensitivity only 45%-58%.
  • Missing infected obstructed stone; requires antibiotics and urgent urology/IR.
  • Relying on absence of hematuria to rule out stone; 11% have no RBCs.
  • Ignoring alternative diagnoses in flank pain alone; 28% have other pathology.
  • Failing to consider CT when pain is refractory to medications, regardless of age.
