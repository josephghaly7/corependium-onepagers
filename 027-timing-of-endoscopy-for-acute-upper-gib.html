Timing of Endoscopy for Acute Upper GIB

Gastrointestinal · Week 12 · src: Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding
Bottom line: Endoscopy <6h did not lower 30-day mortality vs 6-24h in high-risk UGIB (8.9% vs 6.6%).

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1If hemodynamically unstable (even one hypotensive episode, even if it responds to resuscitation), call endoscopist or transfer for urgent endoscopy ASAP.
↓
2If suspected variceal UGIB, give octreotide 50 μg IV bolus then 50 μg/h infusion and ceftriaxone 1 g IV or ciprofloxacin 400 mg IV.
↓
3While awaiting endoscopy, resuscitate aggressively with blood to a reasonable BP; avoid supernormal targets; reverse anticoagulation.
↓
4If hemodynamically stable, calculate Glasgow-Blatchford score (validated; includes hemoglobin, BUN, systolic BP, sex, heart rate, melena, cardiac failure, history of heart disease).
↓
5If Glasgow-Blatchford score >0, consult endoscopist; higher score means sooner assessment.
↓
6If Glasgow-Blatchford score ≥6, push to get endoscopist to patient or patient to endoscopist ASAP.
↓
7Do not delay consult or transfer; high-risk patients can decompensate rapidly.
↓
8Trial inclusion required age ≥18, overt acute UGIB, and Glasgow-Blatchford score ≥12; results apply to this high-risk, mostly peptic ulcer (60%) population, not variceal (8%).

📝 Key Facts

  • Trial randomized high-risk UGIB patients to endoscopy <6h vs 6–24h; 30-day mortality 8.9% vs 6.6%, not statistically significant.
  • Patients in shock or not stabilizing after initial resuscitation were excluded; results do not apply to sickest UGIB patients.
  • Inclusion required age ≥18, overt acute UGIB, and Glasgow-Blatchford score ≥12 (cutoff based on a 2011 Asian study showing significant mortality increase at that score).
  • Only 28% of patients randomized to urgent endoscopy actually received it within 6 hours; 72% got it over next 18 hours.
  • About 8% of delayed endoscopy group decompensated and were switched to endoscopy within 6 hours.
  • Glasgow-Blatchford score >0 predicts need for transfusion, surgery, or endoscopy; score ≥6 corresponds to ~50% intervention rate.
  • About 60% of trial patients bled from peptic ulcers and only ~8% from varices.
  • Over 75% of cases of massive GI bleeding are due to an upper GI source.

Attending Pearls

  • Hemodynamically unstable UGIB, even one responsive hypotension episode, needs immediate endoscopy or transfer.
  • For stable UGIB, run Glasgow-Blatchford; score >0 warrants GI consult, higher score sooner.
  • Resuscitate aggressively with blood to reasonable BP, not supernormal; reverse anticoagulation.
  • Close observation and reevaluation are critical because these patients can decompensate anytime.
  • Don't delay the consult or transfer even if it's the middle of the night; advocate for your patient.

Pitfalls

  • Applying trial results to unstable or shock patients, since they were explicitly excluded.
  • Using this study to delay endoscopy or transfer; high-risk patients can decompensate rapidly.
  • Assuming urgent group truly got <6h endoscopy; only 28% did, so mortality comparison is flawed.
  • Ignoring Glasgow-Blatchford score or delaying GI consult in stable patients with score >0.
