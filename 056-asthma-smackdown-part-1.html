Asthma Smackdown: Part 1

Pediatrics · Week 23 · src: Asthma in Adults
Bottom line: Severe pediatric asthma: treat aggressively upfront; give IM epi 0.01 mg/kg, magnesium 50 mg/kg.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1On arrival, give the kitchen sink: nebs, anticholinergics, IV steroids, and IM epinephrine.
↓
2Give IM epinephrine or terbutaline 0.01 mg/kg/dose, max 0.4 mg; aim higher if unsure.
↓
3Use autoinjector: 0.15 mg for very small child, 0.3 mg if not clearly small; breakpoint ~15-20 kg.
↓
4Give magnesium 50 mg/kg IV (range 25-75 mg/kg), max 2 g, over 20 minutes.
↓
5Do not delay magnesium for fear of hypotension; no routine fluid bolus needed.
↓
6Ignore VBG pH/PCO2; guide by clinical status, respiratory rate, effort, and mental status.
↓
7If tiring, start noninvasive support; high-flow nasal cannula upfront then BiPAP, or straight to BiPAP.
↓
8Start BiPAP low, e.g., 15 over 5; escalate for lethargy or deterioration.
↓
9If clinical deterioration despite support, prepare for intubation.
↓
10Reassess clinically; do not manage by repeat VBGs.

📝 Key Facts

  • IM epinephrine and terbutaline are both dosed 0.01 mg/kg/dose, max 0.4 mg.
  • Autoinjector breakpoint is around 15-20 kg; if uncertain, give 0.3 mg.
  • Magnesium dose range 25-75 mg/kg; choose 50 mg/kg, max 2 g.
  • Magnesium hypotension is diastolic from smooth muscle relaxation; caution does not justify delay or fluid bolus.
  • Podcast summary: magnesium IV 50 mg/kg, steroids IV, IM epinephrine/terbutaline 0.01 mg/kg up to 0.4 mg.
  • High-flow nasal cannula evidence in pediatric asthma is sparse; meta-analysis in bronchiolitis/pneumonia showed higher treatment failure vs CPAP.
  • No PCO2 value should change upfront management; clinical signs precede blood gas changes.

Attending Pearls

  • Be aggressive upfront with the kitchen sink for these sorts of cases.
  • IM epinephrine buys time while you locate albuterol.
  • Magnesium is excellent; do not delay it for fear of hypotension.
  • Treat the child, not the numbers; clinical status drives management.
  • Don't bother with high flow later; just move right on to BiPAP.

Pitfalls

  • Dilly-dallying with small doses prolongs suffering and may worsen outcomes.
  • Relying only on nebulized medications when the chest is quiet; use parenteral route.
  • Managing by repeat VBGs instead of clinical status.
  • Using high-flow nasal cannula late instead of BiPAP may delay needed support.
  • Delaying magnesium for fear of hypotension or giving fluid bolus first.
