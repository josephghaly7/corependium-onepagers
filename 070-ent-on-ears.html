ENT on Ears

HEENT · Week 28 · src: CorePendium Acute Otitis Media, Approach to Ear Pain
Bottom line: Get a good ear exam, hold otoscope upside down, don't overcall AOM, use drops for tubes/perforations.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Restrain child on parent's lap: parent hugs legs, holds arms with one arm and head with other.
↓
2Hold otoscope upside down with handle in thumb crook; pull pinna outward to straighten canal.
↓
3Look through otoscope while advancing, following anterior-superior canal trajectory.
↓
4Assess TM contour and translucency; bulging with lost contour indicates AOM.
↓
5If uncertain about effusion, use pneumatic otoscopy; reduced mobility suggests middle ear fluid.
↓
6If cannot see eardrum due to wax, don't diagnose AOM; arrange ENT follow-up next day.
↓
7If AOM with perforation/drainage, add antibiotic ear drops to oral amoxicillin 80-90 mg/kg/day.
↓
8If tympanostomy tube with active drainage, treat with topical fluoroquinolone drops only, no oral antibiotics.
↓
9If dry inorganic foreign body and first removal attempt fails, stop and refer to ENT.
↓
10If ear pain with normal TM, consider sore throat, TMJ dysfunction, or Eustachian tube dysfunction.

⏱ Time Targets

ENT follow-up when uncertainnext dayif cannot see eardrum or unsure of diagnosis

💊 Doses

amoxicillin80-90 mg/kg/day POfor acute otitis media without perforation
ofloxacinotic dropsfor perforated AOM or draining tube; fluoroquinolone
ciprofloxacin/dexamethasoneotic dropsfor perforated AOM with bleeding; steroid helps granulation tissue
ofloxacinotic dropsfor draining ear tube; if no blood or granulation tissue

📝 Key Facts

  • AOM otoscopic findings: bulging TM, opacification or erythema, reduced mobility with pneumotoscopy.
  • OME is fluid without infection; most resolve spontaneously in 2-3 weeks.
  • Normal TM is conical, translucent; AOM causes bulging, loss of contour, may be red or white.
  • Light reflex only means light is reflecting; can be present with effusion, AOM, or cholesteatoma.
  • Most ear pain is not ear infection; sore throat, TMJ inflammation, and Eustachian tube dysfunction mimic AOM.
  • Spontaneous AOM perforations usually heal on their own.
  • With tympanostomy tubes, AOM causes less pain because pressure is relieved; drainage is the main sign.
  • Dry inorganic foreign bodies can wait for ENT; first attempt is best.

Attending Pearls

  • Ears are hard; the only way to get good is to look at a million ears.
  • Look on the way in with the otoscope to avoid hitting the canal and losing your view.
  • Pneumatic otoscopy helps distinguish effusion; a normal TM snaps back, effusion looks like Saran wrap over Jell-O.
  • If unsure, say 'I don't know' and arrange ENT follow-up; a day of amoxicillin doesn't kill anybody.

Pitfalls

  • Relying on light reflex to rule out AOM; it can be present with effusion, AOM, or cholesteatoma.
  • Diagnosing AOM solely because TM is red; crying can make a normal TM red.
  • Repeatedly attempting removal of a dry inorganic ear foreign body after first failure; risks trauma and loses trust.
  • Using ototoxic drops (polymyxin/neomycin) with ear tubes or perforation; use fluoroquinolone drops instead.
