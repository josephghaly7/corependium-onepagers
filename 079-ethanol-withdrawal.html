Ethanol Withdrawal

Toxicology · Week 37 · 12 min
Bottom line: Severe alcohol withdrawal needs massive sedation, supportive care, and early airway control.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Start with ABCs: IV access, oxygen, cardiac monitor.
↓
2Check bedside glucose; if hypoglycemic, give dextrose.
↓
3Place large-bore IVs and give fluids, preferably glucose-containing.
↓
4Give thiamine, glucose, and folate as adjuncts.
↓
5Start diazepam 10 mg IV and repeat until patient is calm.
↓
6If refractory, add phenobarbital 130 mg; if critically ill, start with 260 mg and repeat 130 mg.
↓
7If intubating, choose GABAergic agents like benzodiazepines or propofol and avoid succinylcholine.
↓
8If intubated, start a propofol drip for ongoing sedation.
↓
9Reassess frequently; anticipate severe autonomic swings and need for airway.

💊 Doses

Diazepam10 mg IVInitial sedation for alcohol withdrawal; repeat as needed.

📝 Key Facts

  • Alcohol withdrawal is a spectrum from anxiety and jitteriness to seizures and death.
  • Delirium tremens is severe withdrawal with altered interaction with the environment and can be fatal.
  • Alcohol level is not helpful; withdrawal can occur at high levels, e.g., dropping from 600 to 200.
  • CIWA and SUSE are scoring tools; SUSE is preferred because CIWA is subjective.
  • Severe autonomic dysfunction can cause systolic BP to swing from over 220 to 90.
  • Check glucose before attributing seizures to withdrawal; patients are often hypoglycemic.

Attending Pearls

  • Treatment is enormous sedation plus supportive care; get ahead of the disease.
  • These patients can be unbelievably ill and truly life-threatening.
  • Use GABAergic agents for RSI; avoid succinylcholine if prolonged seizures or hyperkalemia are concerns.
  • If you have to take their airway, sometimes you have to take their airway.

Pitfalls

  • Under-treatment is the biggest pitfall; patients can deteriorate rapidly.
  • Stopping sedation because doses seem excessive; give until the patient is calm.
  • Forgetting to evaluate for trauma, infection, or other causes of altered mental status.
  • Assuming seizures are from withdrawal without checking glucose.
