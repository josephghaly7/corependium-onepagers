Medical Legal Briefs: Soft Tissue Infections

Dermatology · Week 49 · src: Skin and Soft Tissue Infections
Bottom line: NSTI can mimic DVT; type 2 strep A produces no gas, so absence of gas does not rule out NSTI.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1For leg pain, maintain broad differential: DVT, superficial venous thrombosis, cellulitis, abscess, NSTI, cramping, compartment syndrome, arterial thrombus, venous obstruction.
↓
2If DVT suspected and Doppler unavailable after hours, give Lovenox and arrange morning Doppler.
↓
3If patient returns with worsening pain, re-examine for unilateral swelling and tenderness.
↓
4If Doppler negative but pain persists or worsens, obtain CT and CK to evaluate for myositis or NSTI.
↓
5If CT shows diffuse subcutaneous and intramuscular fascial edema and CK is elevated, consider NSTI even without gas.
↓
6If clinical deterioration occurs, escalate to higher level of care and transfer.
↓
7Document medical decision-making, including absence of suspected infection and sepsis when no source.
↓
8Include the worst diagnosis in the differential to avoid missing NSTI.

📝 Key Facts

  • Wells score of 0 still carries 5% DVT risk; score of 1 up to 17%.
  • ACEP 2018 chest pain guidelines define acceptable miss rate of 1-2%; lower may cause harm.
  • Four types of NSTI; only two produce gas; type 2 is monomicrobial strep A without gas.
  • Absence of gas on exam, POCUS, or CT does not rule out NSTI.
  • NSTI is universally fatal if not diagnosed early.
  • Initial vitals: temp 100.4, HR 126, BP 104/66, RR 14, SpO2 100%; bicarb 23, creatinine 1.4, D-dimer 1140.
  • Return vitals: temp 97.5, BP 122/90, HR 124, RR 18, SpO2 96% on room air; CT at 10 a.m. showed diffuse subcutaneous and intramuscular fascial edema involving all compartments of right lower extremity, most compatible with myositis.
  • In the operating room, signs consistent with necrotizing soft tissue infection were found; patient died several hours later in ICU due to sepsis and multi-organ failure.

Attending Pearls

  • You always have to include the worst diagnosis in your differential or else you won't diagnose it.
  • If you don't diagnose NSTI early, it's universally fatal.
  • Absence of gas does not rule out NSTI.
  • Make your own decision on settle vs trial based on values, confidence, priorities, and risk tolerance.
  • Have your personal attorney draft a hammer letter demanding they do what you want and then if they don't, it opens them up to liability from you if the outcome is harmful to you.

Pitfalls

  • Failing to document medical decision-making, especially absence of sepsis when no source.
  • Assuming absence of gas rules out necrotizing soft tissue infection.
  • Not including worst diagnosis in differential.
  • Being pushed into settlement or trial by pride, lawyer, co-defendants, or insurance company.
  • Not documenting that pain was out of proportion to the suspected diagnosis.
