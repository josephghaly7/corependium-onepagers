Isolated Head Trauma

Trauma · Week 16 · 13 min
Bottom line: Hypotensive brain bleed: support BP immediately with vasopressors, then find cause.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1If isolated head trauma with brain bleed is hypotensive, start norepinephrine drip or push-dose epinephrine immediately.
↓
2While pressors are prepared, give push-dose epinephrine to avoid any period of hypotension.
↓
3Target MAP at least 80; assume ICP ~20 to maintain CPP 55-60.
↓
4Consider hypertonic saline: 3% NaCl or 1-2 amps sodium bicarbonate over ~5 min per amp.
↓
5Search for missed hemorrhage, herniation, iatrogenic hypotension, neurogenic shock, or stress cardiomyopathy.
↓
6For post-intubation sedation, prefer propofol; start infusion at 20-30 mcg/kg/min and titrate.
↓
7Avoid large propofol boluses; if needed, give 20 mg at a time.
↓
8If starting propofol drip without bolus, flush IV dead space with small push to ensure delivery.
↓
9For traumatic ICH, give levetiracetam seizure prophylaxis for 7 days then stop.
↓
10Do not give seizure prophylaxis for spontaneous or aneurysmal bleeds.

💊 Doses

sodium bicarbonate1-2 amps IV over ~5 minutes per amp7% hypertonic saline; lowers ICP and augments cardiac output in hypotensive brain bleed
propofolInfusion 20-30 mcg/kg/min; small boluses 20 mg IV if neededPost-intubation sedation for neurocritical patient; avoid large boluses

📝 Key Facts

  • Isolated head trauma should cause hypertension; hypotension suggests missed injury, herniation, iatrogenic cause, or cardiomyopathy.
  • MAP 80 is bare minimum; with ICP ~20, CPP is 55-60.
  • Mannitol is a potent diuretic; must match urine output with normal saline to prevent hypotension.
  • Brain bleeds can cause stress cardiomyopathy (takotsubo) leading to cardiogenic shock.
  • Seizure prophylaxis for traumatic ICH is levetiracetam for 7 days; not for spontaneous/aneurysmal bleeds.
  • Propofol decreases cerebral metabolic rate and ICP and is eminently titratable.

Attending Pearls

  • Hypotensive brain bleed is dangerous; episodes of hypotension are killers in severe brain injury.
  • If propofol causes vasodilation, eliminate that problem by starting a vasoconstrictor.
  • Exam preservation is less important than using sedatives that decrease cerebral metabolic rate and ICP.
  • When starting a drip without bolus, push just enough to overcome IV dead space.

Pitfalls

  • Leaving hypotensive brain bleed untreated while waiting for fluids; start vasopressors immediately.
  • Giving mannitol without replacing urine output, causing hypovolemia and hypotension.
  • Using large propofol boluses causing wide BP swings; start low and titrate.
  • Starting propofol drip without accounting for IV dead space, leaving patient under-sedated.
