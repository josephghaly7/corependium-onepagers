Miscarriage Care In the ED

OB/GYN · Week 42 · src: CorePendium Approach to Abdominal Pain in Pregnancy, Early Pregnancy Loss
Bottom line: Miscarriage: 1 in 4 recognized pregnancies; confirm nonviable IUP first, then offer expectant, medical, or procedural management.

📝 Key Facts

  • Miscarriage occurs in 10-14% of clinically recognized pregnancies; about 20% or 1 in 5 recognized pregnancies end in miscarriage.
  • Nonviable on single transvaginal ultrasound: crown-rump length >=7 mm without heartbeat or mean sac diameter >=25 mm without embryo.
  • If no IUP and beta hCG <2000, obtain serial beta hCG and ultrasounds; if beta hCG >3500, treat as ectopic.
  • Expectant management success ~80%, average 9 days; 20% need medication or uterine aspiration.
  • Misoprostol alone success ~70% within 7-8 days; mifepristone plus misoprostol success ~85% under 7 days.
  • Manual uterine aspiration success >99%; definitive treatment, no return for same miscarriage, discharge 15 minutes later.
  • Over 180 labor and delivery units have closed across the US since the early 2000s.
  • Nearly one million miscarriage cases present to the ED every year.

Attending Pearls

  • Use direct language: 'You have had a miscarriage. The pregnancy that you have is no longer alive. I'm sorry this happened.'
  • Our job isn't simply to rule out the ectopic pregnancy, but to support the patient with what they need.
  • Patients who receive medical management are less likely to have return ED visits.
  • ED physicians performing uterine aspiration need local OBGYN collaboration and hospital credentialing.
  • Many patients will select expectant management, but spell out all options right away so patient can choose what's best for them.

Pitfalls

  • Failing to offer any management for stable nonviable IUP after ruling out ectopic pregnancy.
  • Using outdated terms like threatened, inevitable, or missed; they don't guide ED clinical decisions.
  • Sending patients home without discussing return precautions, follow-up, or that misoprostol can cause fever lasting <8 hours.
  • Forgetting mifepristone REMS requirements: patient medication guide and provider paperwork.
  • Not asking about safe place for bleeding, travel, housing insecurity, or follow-up access before choosing management.
