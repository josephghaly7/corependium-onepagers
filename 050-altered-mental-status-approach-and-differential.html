Altered Mental Status: Approach and Differential

Neurology · Week 19 · 43 min
Bottom line: Approach altered mental status with ABCs, glucose, temperature, CT, and reversible causes.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Start with IV, O2, monitor, advanced airway equipment; assess ABCs.
↓
2Check fingerstick glucose immediately; if low (<60), give D50.
↓
3Maintain C-spine precautions until trauma is excluded.
↓
4If hypoxic, supplement oxygen; if opioid overdose suspected, give naloxone 0.4 mg increments.
↓
5Obtain early core rectal temperature; treat hypo/hyperthermia emergently.
↓
6Perform disability exam: GCS, pupils, movement of all four extremities.
↓
7Expose patient completely; look for occult trauma, wounds, abscesses.
↓
8Order stat tests: glucose, urine pregnancy, hemoglobin, EKG; send routine labs/CXR.
↓
9Give empiric antibiotics within 1 hour if sepsis/meningitis suspected; add dexamethasone if meningitis.
↓
10Obtain non-contrast CT head; if stable, consider LP after CT; if unstable, defer.

💊 Doses

D50 (dextrose 50%)50 mL IV (25 g)Hypoglycemia; repeat or give 2 amps if profound.
D10 (dextrose 10%)2.5 mL/kg IVPediatric hypoglycemia.
Naloxone0.4 mg IV increments; 2 mg IV for cardiac arrestOpioid-induced respiratory depression.
Thiamine100 mg IVSuspected alcohol/thiamine deficiency.
Ceftriaxone2 g IVSuspected meningitis/sepsis.
Dexamethasone10 mg IVSuspected meningitis, give with or before antibiotics.
Nicardipine10 mg/hr IV dripSevere hypertension in suspected intracranial bleed.

📝 Key Facts

  • AEIOU TIPS: A alcohol/acidosis; E endocrine/epilepsy/electrolytes/encephalopathy; I infection; O opiates/overdose; U uremia; T trauma; I insulin/ischemia; P poisoning/psychosis; S stroke/seizure/syncope.
  • Check fingerstick glucose within first 5 minutes; hypoglycemia is easily reversible.
  • One amp D50 = 50 mL 50% glucose = 25 g; repeat or give 2 amps if profoundly hypoglycemic.
  • Pediatric hypoglycemia: D10 2.5 mL/kg; glucagon ineffective without liver glycogen stores.
  • Naloxone 0.4 mg increments for opioid-induced respiratory depression; 2 mg if cardiac arrest.
  • Give empiric antibiotics within 1 hour for suspected sepsis/meningitis; add dexamethasone 10 mg if meningitis.

Attending Pearls

  • Glucose is basically a vital sign; check early and recheck after treatment.
  • LP is never an emergency; stabilize first, give antibiotics, get CT.
  • Trauma and medical are not exclusive; a stroke can cause a fall and fracture.
  • Use toxicologic physical exam: pupils, skin, bowel sounds, bladder to narrow toxins.

Pitfalls

  • Assuming altered mental status is just alcohol intoxication; chronic alcoholics are high risk for subdural, intracranial bleeds, and meningitis.
  • Delaying core temperature or using temporal thermometer; need early rectal temp.
  • Missing occult trauma; fully expose patient and check axilla, buttocks, posterior neck.
  • Labeling patient psychotic without full medical workup; psychosis is a diagnosis of exclusion in ED.
