Asthma

Pulmonary · Week 10 · src: Asthma in Adults
Bottom line: Avoid intubation in asthma; intubate only if tiring, ventilate 6 mL/kg at half normal rate.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Supplemental oxygen titrated to SpO2 >=90%; avoid hyperoxia which can affect respiratory drive.
↓
2Albuterol MDI 4-12 puffs with spacer every 20 min for 3 treatments; or nebulized 2.5-5 mg per treatment.
↓
3Add ipratropium 0.5 mg nebulized or 8 puffs MDI with spacer for moderate to severe exacerbations.
↓
4Frequently reassess; if symptoms persist, repeat 3 albuterol treatments or continuous neb starting 10 mg/hour and titrate up.
↓
5Give systemic corticosteroids early: PO if able, IV if not; give in all but mildest cases.
↓
6If refractory after steroids, consider magnesium sulfate 2 g IV over 20-30 min as continuous infusion.
↓
7Consider systemic beta-2 agonist rescue: epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg IM anterolateral thigh, repeat every 20 min as needed.
↓
8If refractory after 3 epinephrine doses, start continuous epinephrine infusion 0.01-0.5 mcg/kg/min (1-50 mcg/min), titrate every 2-5 min.
↓
9Consider heliox-driven albuterol (80:20 helium:oxygen) to reduce likelihood of respiratory failure.
↓
10For cooperative patients, start non-invasive ventilation: IPAP 8 cm H2O, EPAP 3 cm H2O, increment with frequent reassessment.

📝 Key Facts

  • Set tidal volume 6 mL/kg at a rate approximately half the normal rate for the patient's age.
  • Change inspiratory:expiratory time to 1:3 or longer when mechanically ventilating.
  • Keep PaCO2 <90-100 mm Hg (<12-13.3 kPa) and pH >7.15-7.2 when ventilating with permissive hypercapnia.
  • Dexamethasone severe exacerbation/status asthmaticus: 16 mg IV adults; 0.6 mg/kg IV pediatrics (max 16 mg).
  • Dexamethasone mild-moderate exacerbation: 0.3-0.6 mg/kg IV as a single dose.
  • Terbutaline maximum total dose is 0.75 mg in 1 hour.
  • Heliox typically given in an 80:20 helium:oxygen ratio.
  • Not all wheezing is asthma; consider mimics including heart failure, PE, anaphylaxis, pneumonia, bronchiolitis, foreign bodies.

Attending Pearls

  • In asthma, intubation should ideally be avoided when possible.
  • Intubation decision is based on clinical assessment and response to therapy.
  • Management of severe asthma requires aggressive, stepwise escalation of treatments.
  • Most patients can be treated easily in the ED and discharged home.
  • Consider ketamine 2 mg/kg as induction agent of choice for RSI in severe, life-threatening asthma.

Pitfalls

  • Avoid hyperoxia; it can affect respiratory drive and a patient can have SpO2 100% with worsening ventilation.
  • Magnesium sulfate may cause hypotension; if it occurs, give an IV isotonic fluid bolus.
  • Epinephrine may cause peripheral vasoconstriction and result in a falsely low oxygen saturation reading.
  • If refractory after 3 doses of IM epinephrine, do not continue bolus dosing; start continuous infusion.
