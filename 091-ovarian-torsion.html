Ovarian Torsion

OB/GYN · Week 41 · src: Ovarian Pathology, Testicular Torsion
Bottom line: Ovarian torsion: suspect in all ages; normal exam, arterial Doppler, or long symptom duration does not exclude.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Suspect torsion regardless of age or pregnancy; mean age 29-33 years.
↓
2Assess risk factors: enlarged ovary >4-5 cm, fertility therapy, PCOS, tubal ligation, prior torsion.
↓
3Do not use normal abdominal, pelvic, or bimanual exam to exclude torsion.
↓
4If suspicious, consult OBGYN before imaging; if OBGYN unavailable, consult general surgeon on call.
↓
5Adults: transvaginal ultrasound with grayscale and Doppler; children: transabdominal ultrasound.
↓
6Interpret ultrasound using combination: ovary >4 cm, free pelvic fluid, midline ovary, vascular flow.
↓
7Normal arterial flow does not exclude torsion; up to two-thirds have detectable arterial flow.
↓
8If CT done, specific findings (absent enhancement, whirlpool sign, displaced follicles) rule in torsion.
↓
9Completely normal CT of pelvis (normal size, no mass, no fluid, no displacement) approaches 100% sensitivity to exclude.
↓
10Do not withhold surgery for prolonged symptoms; viability reported up to 140-160 hours.

📝 Key Facts

  • Mean age 29-33 years; 15% infancy/childhood (over half have normal ovaries); postmenopausal 15-25% (mostly have underlying pelvic mass).
  • Enlarged ovary >4-5 cm is most common risk factor; 80-85% have associated cyst or mass.
  • Fertility therapy causes ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome and large cysts; 8-12% of these patients experience torsion.
  • Prior torsion recurrence up to 11%; PCOS and tubal ligation are also risk factors.
  • Pain present in 90% but 50% lack sudden severe pain; nausea/vomiting 47-70%; fever 2-20%.
  • Up to 30% have no abdominal/pelvic tenderness; EP interexaminer reliability: pelvic mass <25%, adnexal tenderness <32%; OBGYN sensitivity for adnexal mass >5 cm 15-36%.
  • Normal-sized ovary in 15% of all torsion cases; 50% in pediatric cases.
  • Arterial flow normal in 25-54% of OR-confirmed torsion; sensitivity of ultrasound ranges 35% to 90s; specificity near 100% with combined findings.

Attending Pearls

  • Ovarian torsion affects women of all ages.
  • Do not rely on a normal pelvic or bimanual examination to rule out torsion.
  • Normal arterial flow on Doppler ultrasound cannot rule out torsion.
  • A normal abdominal pelvic CT significantly decreases the likelihood of torsion; certain CT findings are highly suggestive.
  • Patients may have symptoms for several days and still have viable ovaries after surgery.

Pitfalls

  • Relying on classic acute severe pain and vomiting; half have gradual, intermittent, or mild pain.
  • Using normal pelvic/bimanual exam to rule out torsion; up to 30% have no tenderness.
  • Using normal arterial Doppler to rule out torsion; arterial flow normal in 25-54%.
  • Assuming prolonged symptoms mean nonviable ovary; viable ovary can persist for days.
  • Delaying OBGYN consultation for diagnostic testing in hemodynamically compromised or suspected torsion; consultation should not be delayed.
