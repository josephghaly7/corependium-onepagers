Treating Victims of Sexual Assault

Residency Fundamentals · Week 1 · 19 min
Bottom line: ED role: stabilize, preserve evidence, offer prophylaxis, psychosocial support, safety plan.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Prioritize patient and medically stabilize; evaluate acute traumatic injuries first.
↓
2If no forensic exam capability, transfer after medical clearance to appropriate facility.
↓
3Assemble team: forensic nurse examiner, social work, mental health, law enforcement as appropriate.
↓
4Document history objectively, brief, targeted to assault details and injury risk.
↓
5Perform focused physical exam minimizing exposure; defer pelvic exam to forensic examiner unless acute injury concern.
↓
6Obtain pregnancy test and STI testing per CDC: chlamydia, gonorrhea, trichomonas, HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis.
↓
7Offer STI prophylaxis: gonorrhea, chlamydia, trichomonas; hepatitis B immunization; HPV if age eligible.
↓
8Discuss HIV PEP case-by-case; use national PEP line 888-HIV-4911 if uncertain.
↓
9Offer pregnancy prophylaxis with levonorgestrel within 72 hours; update tetanus.
↓
10Arrange follow-up, safety plan, and resources; consider trafficking and arrange support.

📝 Key Facts

  • Drug-facilitated sexual assault occurs in up to 50% of cases; toxicology testing ideally within 72 hours.
  • HIV follow-up testing recommended at 6 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months post-assault.
  • Levonorgestrel/Plan B ideally given within 72 hours of sexual assault.
  • HPV vaccination recommended for female survivors age 9-26 and males age 9-21.
  • Forensic evidence collection is time sensitive; prioritize and medically clear patients ASAP.
  • No current CDC guidelines for hepatitis C testing; decide case-by-case.

Attending Pearls

  • Defer pelvic exam to forensic nurse examiner unless acute injury concern.
  • Offer antiemetic with prophylaxis, especially HIV PEP, to improve tolerance.
  • Keep clothing on and minimize exposure to preserve evidence and reduce trauma.
  • Ensure safety plan and resources before discharge; trust improves adherence.

Pitfalls

  • Repeating patient's story multiple times or documenting conflicting details; keep HPI brief and objective.
  • Performing unnecessary pelvic exam before forensic exam, risking trauma and evidence loss.
  • Using medical jargon in documentation that a lay jury may not understand.
  • Missing signs of recurrent abuse, domestic violence, or human trafficking.
