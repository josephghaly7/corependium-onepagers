Major Head Trauma

Trauma · Week 16 · src: Pediatric Head Trauma major and minor, Pediatric Major Trauma Assessment
Bottom line: Prevent secondary brain injury via ABCs, SBP/oxygenation; GCS<=8 intubate, no prophylactic hyperventilation.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Start with ABCs; treat other life threats before focusing on head.
↓
2Maintain spinal precautions/C-spine in all head trauma.
↓
3Airway: intubate for GCS <=8; oxygen saturation >92% and <98%.
↓
4Ventilate: target PaCO2 35-45 mm Hg, ETCO2 30-40 mm Hg; correlate capnography with blood gas early.
↓
5Avoid prophylactic hyperventilation.
↓
6Circulation: prevent hypotension; treat with isotonic fluids, blood products, vasopressors.
↓
7Disability: if elevated ICP/impending herniation, head of bed 30 degrees.
↓
8Give hypertonic 3% NaCl 5 mL/kg IV over 15 min or mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg IV over 20-30 min.
↓
9Maintain normal glucose and normothermia; give seizure prophylaxis for moderate to severe TBI.
↓
10If seizures on scene or arrival, treat as status epilepticus; CT head and neurosurgery consult.

📝 Key Facts

  • Oxygen saturation target: >92% and <98%.
  • Endotracheal intubation and controlled ventilation for GCS <=8.
  • Target PaCO2 35-45 mm Hg and end-tidal CO2 30-40 mm Hg.
  • Hypotension lower limits: SBP <60 term neonates (0-28 days); <70 infants (1-12 months); <70 + (2 age) children 1-10 years; <90 children >=10 years; not therapeutic targets.
  • Admission SBP below the 75th percentile after severe TBI is associated with elevated in-hospital mortality.
  • Cerebral perfusion pressure = MAP - ICP; in severe head trauma ICP can rise to 20-30, even 40 mm Hg.
  • GCS range 3-15, developed in Glasgow in 1974; motor score most important (6 follows commands, 5 localizes pain, 4 withdraws, 3 decorticate flexion, 2 decerebrate extension, 1 none).
  • Children <4 years old and those with hypotension, low GCS on presentation, coagulopathy, or hyperglycemia have highest morbidity and mortality.

Attending Pearls

  • Basic emergency care—airway and blood pressure—has proven best; many targeted neuroprotective therapies failed over the last 20 years.
  • Serial GCS changes matter more than any single number; GCS 15 can have impending death in an hour.
  • Since ED can't measure ICP, manage BP and oxygenation until ICU.
  • Cushing response: extreme hypertension compensates for very high ICP to perfuse the brain.
  • GCS is a blunt force instrument, not a neurological exam; motor score is what neurosurgeons rely on most.

Pitfalls

  • Prophylactic hyperventilation is not recommended.
  • Focusing on head injury while ignoring ABCs and other life threats; think patient whole.
  • Intubating solely for GCS <8 without considering seizure, intoxication, or trajectory; GCS 15 can have impending herniation.
  • Treating hypotension threshold as therapeutic target; thresholds not targets.
  • Do not rely on hypotension alone to identify shock in pediatric trauma; tachycardia or poor perfusion may be only clues.
