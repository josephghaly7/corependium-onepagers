Thyroid Emergencies

Endocrine · Week 45 · src: Thyroid Storm and Hyperthyroidism, Decompensated Hypothyroidism
Bottom line: Thyroid storm and myxedema coma are clinical diagnoses; treat without waiting for labs.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Thyroid storm: secure airway; if large goiter, suspect distortion and downsize to 7.0 ETT.
↓
2Thyroid storm: consider increased tidal volume and respiratory rate due to hypermetabolic state.
↓
3Thyroid storm: fluid resuscitation, then propylthiouracil 500-1,000 mg PO once, then 250 mg q4h.
↓
4Thyroid storm: POCUS heart before beta-blockade; esmolol 250-500 µg/kg IV bolus then 50-100 µg/kg/min.
↓
5Titrate esmolol by 25-50 µg/kg/min every 5 min until symptoms resolve (max 300 µg/kg/min).
↓
6Thyroid storm: hydrocortisone 300 mg IV load, then 100 mg IV q8h; give iodine at least 60 min after antithyroid med.
↓
7Thyroid storm: treat hyperthermia with passive cooling and acetaminophen; NSAIDs contraindicated.
↓
8Myxedema coma: start treatment if suspected; consider hypoglycemia, hypotension, hypothermia, bradycardia, altered mental status.
↓
9Myxedema coma: hydrocortisone 100 mg IV, levothyroxine 200-400 µg IV over 2-4 min (max 100 µg/min), consider liothyronine 5-20 µg IV.
↓
10Myxedema coma: rewarm, monitor, and give vasopressors only after steroids and thyroid hormone.

📝 Key Facts

  • Thyroid storm: antithyroid meds first, then iodine at least 60 minutes later.
  • Thyroid storm: POCUS cardiac function before beta-blockade.
  • Thyroid storm: NSAIDs are contraindicated for hyperthermia.
  • Myxedema coma: treatment is started if the diagnosis is suspected; do not wait for laboratory confirmation.
  • Myxedema coma: common triggers include pneumonia, urinary tract infections, cellulitis, hypoglycemia, amiodarone, lithium, beta blockers, and medication noncompliance.
  • Myxedema coma: may occur with adrenal insufficiency; treat empirically with IV hydrocortisone.
  • Myxedema coma: CHF may occur and may not respond to vasopressors if thyroid hormone supplementation is not given.
  • Myxedema coma: examine for prior thyroid surgery scars; temporary cardiac pacing may be needed for bradycardia.

Attending Pearls

  • Thyroid storm and myxedema coma are clinical diagnoses; treat based on gestalt, not lab values.
  • In myxedema coma, waiting could be very bad.
  • A large goiter does not mean the patient is thyrotoxic; it may instead mean an airway emergency.
  • You have to be comfortable with being uncomfortable when managing thyroid emergencies.
  • The order of medications in thyroid storm is overblown and can be simplified.

Pitfalls

  • Waiting for laboratory confirmation before treating decompensated hypothyroidism.
  • Giving iodine before or shortly after antithyroid medications instead of at least 60 minutes later.
  • Giving beta-blockade in thyroid storm without first assessing cardiac function with POCUS.
  • Using NSAIDs to treat hyperthermia in thyroid storm.
  • Giving vasopressors before steroids and thyroid hormone in myxedema coma.
