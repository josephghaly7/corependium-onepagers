Thyroid Emergencies

Endocrine · Week 45 · src: Hypertensive Emergencies, Neonatal Emergencies
Bottom line: Thyroid emergencies are clinical diagnoses; treat storm and myxedema coma without waiting for labs.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess clinically for thyroid storm or myxedema coma; do not wait for labs.
↓
2If severe unexplained symptoms, check TSH and free T4.
↓
3If TSH very low or very high, confirm with free T4.
↓
4Recognize high-output heart failure or cardiovascular collapse as decompensation.
↓
5Provide supportive measures and control effects of excess or deficient thyroid hormone.
↓
6Treat the underlying thyroid state to restore balance and prevent deterioration.

📝 Key Facts

  • Graves disease accounts for 60-80% of hyperthyroidism cases.
  • Free T4 is the active form (only 1% of T4 is free); T3 is the active hormone, 80% from peripheral conversion.
  • No lab value excludes thyroid storm or myxedema coma; treatment is clinical.
  • Myxedema coma is the severe, life-threatening end of hypothyroidism.
  • Hyperthyroidism means excess thyroid hormone; thyrotoxicosis is the symptomatic clinical syndrome.
  • Thyroid storm is the severe, life-threatening end of hyperthyroidism.
  • High-output heart failure in hyperthyroidism can have ejection fraction 65-90%.
  • Thyroid hormone increases basal metabolic rate, cardiac output, and causes arterial dilation.

Attending Pearls

  • Treat decompensated thyroid disease based on clinical presentation, not lab values.
  • TSH is a game-changing screening test in unexplained multisystem symptoms.
  • High-output heart failure with supernormal ejection fraction can signal hyperthyroidism.
  • Both thyroid storm and myxedema coma end in cardiovascular collapse if untreated.
  • You have to be comfortable with being uncomfortable because sometimes you treat based on clinical gestalt, and waiting can be very bad.

Pitfalls

  • Waiting for lab confirmation before treating myxedema coma or thyroid storm.
  • Assuming a large goiter means the patient is thyrotoxic.
  • Relying solely on thyroid labs instead of clinical gestalt.
  • Missing thyroid disease in unexplained diarrhea, psychiatric complaints, or proximal weakness.
