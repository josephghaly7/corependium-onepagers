Zapped Part 2: Lightning Strike

Environmental · Week 46 · src: Lightning and Electrical Emergencies
Bottom line: Lightning: 15% mortality, flashover, CPR saves apneic/pulseless, minor burns, trauma is key.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Mass casualty: reverse triage—treat apneic/pulseless lightning victims first.
↓
2For arrest: CPR until perfusing rhythm (heart restarts), ACLS dysrhythmia protocol.
↓
3Fluid: 1-L isotonic crystalloid bolus, repeat to urine output 1-1.5 mL/kg/hour.
↓
4Assume trauma in high-voltage/lightning; assess spine, joints, posterior shoulder dislocations.
↓
5Check tympanic membranes for rupture from thermoacoustic blast wave.
↓
6If not arrest but hypotensive, look hard for trauma (lightning does not cause hypotension).
↓
7Evaluate keraunoparalysis (cold, clammy, pulseless limbs) but reassure it is self-limiting vasospasm.
↓
8If asymptomatic and no arrhythmia, screening EKG is reasonable; no further monitoring needed.
↓
9Minor linear steam burns: expectant management; full-thickness burns suggest metal contact.
↓
10Discharge asymptomatic patients to regional burn center for long-term neuro follow-up.

⏱ Time Targets

Cardiac automaticity recovery60-90 secondsAfter countershock asystole; pacemaker cells intact.
Keraunoparalysis resolution4-6 hoursVasospasm releases, pulses/sensation/motor return.
Lichtenberg ferning fading~4 hoursBenign, no tissue damage.
Lightning current duration2-3 millisecondsExplains flashover and survival.

💊 Doses

Isotonic crystalloid1-L IV bolus, repeat to urine output 1-1.5 mL/kg/hourFor fluid resuscitation; Parkland may underestimate needs.

📝 Key Facts

  • 5-30 million volts DC delivered in 2-3 ms; mortality ~15%.
  • Flashover: vast majority of electrons cascade over body, not through it.
  • T° of bolt ~30,000 K, hotter than sun surface; thermoacoustic blast wave 20-100 atm.
  • Cardiac: countershock into asystole, restarts in 60-90 seconds; brainstem stunning causes longer apnea.
  • Apnea from brainstem stunning outlasts cardiac stunning—CPR saves lives.
  • TM rupture from blast wave when lightning hits 10 feet away.
  • Lightning burns mostly minor linear steam burns; <5% full-thickness, usually from metal contact superheating to ~1000°F in 2 ms.
  • Rhabdo and compartment syndrome extremely rare—electrons don't pass through muscle.
  • Keraunoparalysis (step voltage) in 50-60%: pulseless, pale, insensate legs resolve in 4-6 hours.
  • 75-80% develop long-term neuro sequelae (memory, behavior, depression, chronic pain).
  • Lightning does not cause hypotension; hypotension means occult trauma.

Attending Pearls

  • Flashover effect explains 85% survival and why burns are mostly minor steam burns.
  • CPR can completely save lightning arrest victims because the heart restarts; support through apnea.
  • Keraunoparalysis looks catastrophic but is vasospasm; pulses and sensation return in 4-6 hours.
  • Asymptomatic lightning victims still need burn center follow-up due to high rate of neuro sequelae.
  • People struck by lightning are never the same again—75% have long-term neuro sequelae.

Pitfalls

  • Black-tagging pulseless apneic lightning victims in mass casualty—they need CPR first.
  • Assuming lightning causes rhabdo/compartment syndrome; it rarely does.
  • Attributing hypotension to lightning itself; it's trauma until proven otherwise.
  • Treating Lichtenberg ferning as a burn; it's benign and fades in ~4 hours.
  • Underestimating internal tissue damage based on external burns.
