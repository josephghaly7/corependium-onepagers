Lower GI Bleeding

Gastrointestinal · Week 12 · 26 min
Bottom line: Most lower GI bleeds benign; observe, risk stratify, CTA if active.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Ask for stool photo and whether clots or blood pass without stool.
↓
2Obtain CBC for true hematochezia, concerning vitals, or anemia; not trivial streaks.
↓
3If stable, mild bleeding, and no anemia, consider discharge with GI follow-up.
↓
4If unstable (shock index >1) or hematochezia within 4 hours, order triple-phase CT angiography.
↓
5If CTA shows active extravasation, consult IR first for embolization; notify GI and surgery.
↓
6If CTA negative and stable, observe in ED and reassess for recurrent bleeding.
↓
7If ongoing bleeding with instability, start massive transfusion protocol and resuscitate.
↓
8If exsanguinating with no definitive control, consider Blakemore balloon tamponade as rescue only.
↓
9After stabilization, admit to ICU for continued transfusion and monitoring.
↓
10If refractory bleeding after IR, consult surgery for possible laparotomy/colectomy.

📝 Key Facts

  • Lower GI bleed mortality is 2-4%; upper GI mortality is about five times higher.
  • Most lower GI bleeds are diverticular and stop on their own.
  • ACG guidelines: CTA warranted for unstable patients or hematochezia within last 4 hours.
  • Oakland and SHA2PE scores identify safe discharge: no severe tachycardia, hypotension, or anemia.
  • Rectal exam is often unhelpful between bleeding episodes; blood on exam has little prognostic weight.
  • ACG 2023: for hematochezia with CTA active extravasation, IR should be first-line, not GI.

Attending Pearls

  • Ask patients for a stool photo; it helps distinguish minor from severe bleeding.
  • Observation in ED is powerful; stable patients can suddenly deteriorate, so reassess.
  • For unstable lower GI bleeds, get consultants on one call to avoid hot potato.
  • Risk scores don't capture social determinants; arrange follow-up or admit if barriers exist.

Pitfalls

  • Relying on a negative rectal exam to rule out significant lower GI bleed.
  • Ordering CTA for all hematochezia; low yield if bleeding stopped or minor.
  • Calling GI first for unstable lower GI bleed with active extravasation; IR should be first.
  • Discharging without considering barriers to outpatient colonoscopy and follow-up.
