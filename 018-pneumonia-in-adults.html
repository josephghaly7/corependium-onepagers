Pneumonia in Adults

Pulmonary · Week 9 · src: Community-Acquired Pneumonia
Bottom line: CAP: strep most common; H-CAP gone; give broad antibiotics for septic shock.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Administer supplemental oxygen for hypoxemia; suction for aspiration.
↓
2Provide NIPPV or intubate for hypoxemia/respiratory distress; ARDSNet tidal volume 6-8 mL/kg PBW.
↓
3Give crystalloid bolus 20-30 mL/kg for septic shock; use 250-500 mL if heart failure/oliguric renal failure.
↓
4Start norepinephrine 0.01-0.1 μg/kg/min (or 1-10 μg/min), titrate every 2-5 min.
↓
5Add vasopressin 0.03-0.04 units/min once norepinephrine ≥15 μg/min (max 0.06 units/min).
↓
6Use epinephrine 0.01-0.1 μg/kg/min IV titrated every 2-5 min if needed.
↓
7Give broad-spectrum antibiotics in ED for critically ill CAP patients.
↓
8Choose regimen: azithromycin 500 mg IV q24h + cefepime 2 g IV q8h + vancomycin load 20-35 mg/kg (max 3 g) then 15-20 mg/kg IV q8-12h.
↓
9Alternative: cefepime 2 g IV q8h + doxycycline 100 mg IV q12h + vancomycin as above.
↓
10Alternative: azithromycin 500 mg IV q24h + piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6h + vancomycin as above.

📝 Key Facts

  • CAP: pneumonia contracted outside inpatient hospital setting.
  • HAP: develops at least 2 days after hospitalization.
  • VAP: develops at least 2 days after intubation.
  • H-CAP removed in 2016 IDSA/ATS guidelines.
  • Negative chest X-ray does not exclude CAP.
  • Strep pneumoniae remains most common cause.
  • Staph aureus risk: recent hospitalization, indwelling line, dialysis, recent influenza.
  • Classic strep: rust-colored sputum, gram-positive clusters, rigors, wedge-shaped infiltrate.

Attending Pearls

  • Pneumonia is bread and butter and moon medicine.
  • Secondary staph infection after influenza caused many 1918 flu deaths.
  • Atypical organisms often absent on gram stain.
  • Use clinical judgment about healthcare exposures instead of obsolete H-CAP.
  • Klebsiella: currant jelly sputum in alcoholics.

Pitfalls

  • Don't rely on typical vs atypical presentation to identify organism.
  • Don't miss mimics: PE, ARDS, TB, cancer, right-sided endocarditis.
  • Don't assume hemoptysis is pneumonia; bronchitis most common cause.
  • Don't ignore resistant pathogen risk just because H-CAP removed.
  • Don't use vasopressin as monotherapy for septic shock.
