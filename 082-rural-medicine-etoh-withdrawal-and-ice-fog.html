Rural Medicine: ETOH Withdrawal and Ice Fog

Toxicology · Week 38 · src: Ethanol Withdrawal
Bottom line: Treat withdrawal aggressively early; if >300 mg diazepam, add phenobarbital and prepare airway.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Screen for hypoglycemia; give dextrose 50% 50 mL IV (25 g) through large vein.
↓
2Maintain SpO2 >92%; use nasal cannula to avoid aspiration risk.
↓
3For hypotension/dehydration, give IV fluid boluses (e.g., Ringer lactate).
↓

↓
5If no IV access, use lorazepam or midazolam IM; diazepam avoid IM.
↓
6If refractory to high-dose benzos, give phenobarbital 10 mg/kg IBW IV over 15-30 min; prepare for intubation.
↓
7For persistent agitation/hallucinosis, add antipsychotics (haloperidol, droperidol).
↓
8Treat nausea/vomiting with antiemetics (e.g., ondansetron).
↓
9Give thiamine 500 mg IV every 8 hours for deficiency.
↓
10For transport, if weather/weight unsafe, defer to pilot and land at closest ICU.

📝 Key Facts

  • Severe ethanol withdrawal can be fatal; treat as medical emergency.
  • Patients may have measurable/elevated serum ethanol; these often have more severe withdrawal and may require both benzodiazepine and phenobarbital.
  • Ethanol withdrawal syndrome including delirium tremens is a clinical diagnosis based on history and physical exam.
  • Validated scoring tools (CIWA-Ar, SEWS) guide but should not be rigid protocol-driven.
  • Kindling: each withdrawal episode worsens and may cause benzo resistance; treat early and aggressively.
  • Once point of no return reached, expect 3-4 day trajectory of worsening before improvement.
  • Medevac plane patient limit ~450 lb; stretcher loader limit 400-425 lb; this patient weighed >500 lb.
  • Nurses managed patient one-on-one for ~30 hours before physician arrival; ice fog prevented landing.

Attending Pearls

  • Get on top of withdrawal early; if initial modest benzos control symptoms, you may stave off DTs.
  • Once withdrawal reaches point of no return, expect a 3-4 day trajectory of worsening before improvement.
  • In refractory withdrawal, use multimodal adjuncts (neuroleptics, antiepileptics, ketamine, NG/rectal routes) and call poison center.
  • Pilot's decision is final; closest ICU with a ventilator is appropriate.
  • The ice fog lifted; there was a window for the medevac.

Pitfalls

  • Inadequate treatment with benzodiazepines and/or phenobarbital.
  • Failure to anticipate airway compromise and monitor airway early.
  • Continuing benzo or barbiturate drips after intubation instead of switching to propofol.
  • Overriding pilot's safety judgment or insisting on larger ICU when closest ICU is safer.
  • Assuming withdrawal without considering other diagnoses (subdural, hyponatremia, meningococcemia).
