Rural Medicine: ETOH Withdrawal and Ice Fog

Toxicology · Week 38 · 29 min
Bottom line: Treat alcohol withdrawal aggressively early; use adjuncts and prioritize safe transport.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess for alternate diagnoses (subdural, hyponatremia, meningococcemia) before attributing to withdrawal.
↓
2Assess alcohol withdrawal severity early; start symptom-triggered diazepam 10-20 mg IV and reassess frequently.
↓
3If symptoms persist, double diazepam dose each time; no upper limit until ~300 mg total.
↓
4If refractory to high-dose benzos, add phenobarbital; prepare for airway/respiratory depression.
↓
5If still uncontrolled, consider adjuncts: haloperidol, valproate/gabapentin, ketamine, or NG/rectal routes.
↓
6If resources exhausted, consider alcohol via NG (vodka) with poison center/addiction medicine guidance.
↓
7If intubating, use propofol for sedation rather than benzo/barb drips.
↓
8If transport needed, coordinate medevac; if weather/weight unsafe, defer to pilot and go to closest ICU.

💊 Doses

diazepam10-20 mg IVSymptom-triggered therapy for alcohol withdrawal; may double each dose.

📝 Key Facts

  • Kindling: each withdrawal episode is worse and can cause benzo resistance; treat early and aggressively.
  • Diazepam has active metabolites; symptom-triggered dosing can double up to ~300 mg before reassessing.
  • Phenobarbital plus benzos causes synergistic respiratory depression; prepare for intubation.
  • Medevac plane patient limit ~450 lb; stretcher loader limit 400-425 lb; this patient weighed >500 lb.
  • Nurses managed patient one-on-one for ~30 hours before physician arrival; ice fog prevented landing.
  • After arrival, docs exhausted all benzos in ~2 hours with q5 min dosing; phenobarb had no effect.

Attending Pearls

  • Get on top of withdrawal early; if initial modest benzos control symptoms, you may stave off DTs.
  • Once withdrawal reaches point of no return, expect a 3-4 day trajectory of worsening before improvement.
  • In refractory withdrawal, use multimodal adjuncts (neuroleptics, antiepileptics, ketamine, NG/rectal routes) and call poison center.
  • Pilot's decision is final; closest ICU with a ventilator is appropriate.

Pitfalls

  • Under-treating early withdrawal; be aggressive with benzos from the start to prevent DTs.
  • Continuing benzo or barbiturate drips after intubation instead of switching to propofol.
  • Overriding pilot's safety judgment or insisting on a larger ICU when closest ICU is safer.
  • Assuming withdrawal without considering other diagnoses (subdural, hyponatremia, meningococcemia) in altered patients.
