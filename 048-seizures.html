Seizures

Neurology · Week 18 · src: Adult Seizures
Bottom line: Status epilepticus: seizure ≥5 min or ≥2 without return to baseline; benzodiazepine first.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess airway; have advanced airway equipment ready; intubate if vomiting, apneic, or not protecting airway.
↓
2Give supplemental oxygen as needed.
↓
3Place on monitor and obtain IV access; use IM or intranasal route if IV not immediately available.
↓
4Obtain point-of-care blood glucose in all seizing patients.
↓
5Give benzodiazepine for any seizure lasting ≥5 min: diazepam 10 mg IV over 1-2 min, lorazepam 2-8 mg IV over 1-2 min (4-8 mg IV if critically ill), or midazolam 10 mg IV over 1-2 min.
↓
6If no immediate IV access, give midazolam IM (preferred over other benzodiazepines due to more rapid onset and absorption); avoid diazepam IM (erratic absorption).
↓
7Escalate to second- and third-line agents as needed until seizure aborted.
↓
8For recalcitrant seizures, consider eclampsia, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, isoniazid poisoning, theophylline poisoning.
↓
9Obtain head CT and labs in select patients to address provoked cause; obtain ECG in postictal patients.
↓
10Obtain EEG in patients with prolonged postictal period; consider neurology consultation.

📝 Key Facts

  • Status epilepticus is a seizure lasting ≥5 minutes or ≥2 seizures without a return to baseline between them.
  • Seizures are classified as provoked or unprovoked based on cause and as focal or generalized based on EEG findings.
  • All seizure patients should be evaluated with detailed medical history, neurological exam, point-of-care blood glucose, and pregnancy testing in female patients of childbearing age.
  • First seizures in adults should be evaluated with ECG to exclude cardiogenic syncope, focused laboratory tests to exclude metabolic abnormalities, and a non-contrast head CT in select patients.
  • Fosphenytoin, levetiracetam, and valproic acid are equally effective as second-line medications (Established Status Epilepticus Treatment Trial).
  • Benzodiazepines (diazepam, lorazepam, midazolam) are the initial therapy for status epilepticus.
  • All benzodiazepines can be given intraosseous if needed.
  • Epilepsy affects as many as 1 in 100 people in the world.

Attending Pearls

  • Not all patients with status epilepticus need intubation, but consider it for those who are vomiting, apneic, or not protecting their airway.
  • For recalcitrant seizures, consider eclampsia, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, isoniazid poisoning, theophylline poisoning.
  • Generalized seizures with loss of consciousness are the main ED concern.
  • Having a clear approach in mind makes seizing patients less intimidating.
  • Seizures in epilepsy patients differ from first-ever seizures.

Pitfalls

  • Nonconvulsive status epilepticus should be considered in all patients with prolonged postictal period and/or altered mental status of unknown etiology.
  • Look for and address complications from status epilepticus such as hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, and lactic acidosis.
  • Giving diazepam intramuscularly due to erratic absorption may delay control of seizures.
  • Confusing seizure with convulsions; convulsions are only motor manifestations of a seizure.
  • Treating all seizures the same without considering clinical context (pregnancy, HIV, alcohol, trauma).
