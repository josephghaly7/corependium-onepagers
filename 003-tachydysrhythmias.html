Tachydysrhythmias

Cardiology · Week 2 · 17 min
Bottom line: Five-step approach to tachyarrhythmias: prepare, assess stability, P waves, regularity, width.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Bring IV, O2, monitor, advanced airway equipment, defibrillator, crash cart to bedside for every tachyarrhythmia.
↓
2Assess stability: altered mental status, pulmonary edema, hypotension, chest pain; unstable favors electricity.
↓
3Look for P waves before every QRS; normal axis upright in II, negative in aVR.
↓
4If sinus tachycardia, treat underlying cause (PE, sepsis, etc.), not the rate.
↓
5Assess QRS regularity using calipers or paper notch method; irregular means origin above AV node.
↓
6Assess QRS width: narrow <3 small boxes in adults; wide suggests VT.
↓
7Classify into four groups: narrow regular, narrow irregular, wide regular, wide irregular.
↓
8For irregular/narrow supraventricular rhythms, block AV node; for unstable/wide, prepare electricity.

📝 Key Facts

  • Five-step approach: IV/O2/monitor/airway/defibrillator/crash cart, stable or not, P waves or not, regular or irregular, wide or narrow.
  • Unstable indicators include altered mental status, pulmonary edema, hypotension, and chest pain; chest pain needs nuance.
  • Sinus rhythm: P wave before every QRS, upright in II, negative in aVR; sinus tachycardia rate varies.
  • Irregular rhythm originates above AV node, so it is not VT and is treated by AV node blockade.
  • Narrow QRS is <3 small boxes in adults and indicates supraventricular origin; wide suggests VT.
  • All tachyarrhythmias fall into four groups: narrow regular, narrow irregular, wide regular, wide irregular.

Attending Pearls

  • Stable patients have time to think, examine ECGs, and consult; unstable patients need rapid intervention.
  • More unstable favors electricity; more stable favors avoiding electricity.
  • Irregular equals above AV node, block AV node; irregular equals not VT.
  • Narrow equals above AV node, block AV node; narrow and irregular is very unlikely VT.

Pitfalls

  • Failing to ask 'is this sinus rhythm?' and not looking for P waves can fool you in fast, sick patients.
  • Treating sinus tachycardia as an arrhythmia instead of the underlying cause (PE, sepsis, etc.).
  • Assuming chest pain always means unstable; young SVT patients with chest pain may be stable.
  • Not preparing for deterioration because every antiarrhythmic drug is proarrhythmic and cardioversion can go badly.
