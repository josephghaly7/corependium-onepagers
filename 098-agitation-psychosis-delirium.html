Agitation, Psychosis, Delirium

Psychiatry · Week 43 · src: Anxiety and Panic Disorder, Acute Psychosis, The Suicidal Patient
Bottom line: Agitation/psychosis: verbal de-escalation then sedation; avoid routine urine drug screens.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Search patient for weapons, clear room of objects, supervise for self-injury risk.
↓
2Perform primary survey; check glucose, pulse oximetry, and history as soon as safe.
↓
3Assess for life-threatening causes of agitation and delirium (waxing/waning mental status).
↓
4Attempt verbal de-escalation first, quickly followed by sedation if ineffective.
↓
5Recruit staff and apply physical restraints if de-escalation and sedation fail.
↓
6Reassess need for continued physical restraints at frequent intervals; constant observation if severe.
↓
7For undifferentiated acute psychosis, give antipsychotic +/- benzodiazepine: droperidol, haloperidol, olanzapine, or ziprasidone.
↓
8For severe agitation, consider ketamine 3-4 mg/kg IM or 1 mg/kg slow IV push.
↓
9Avoid routine urine drug screens; test based on history, physical, and epidemiology.
↓
10Place psychiatric hold if danger to self, danger to others, or gravely disabled; know local duration.

📝 Key Facts

  • Droperidol 5-10 mg IM or 2.5-5 mg slow IV push for undifferentiated acute psychosis.
  • Haloperidol 5-10 mg slow IV push or IM; consider monitoring because of dysrhythmia risk.
  • Ketamine 2-6 mg/kg IM, maximum 500 mg; recommended initial 3-4 mg/kg IM.
  • Ketamine 1-2 mg/kg slow IV push; start 1 mg/kg, repeat 0.5 mg/kg as needed.
  • Olanzapine 10 mg IM; avoid concomitant benzodiazepines due to hemodynamic collapse risk.
  • Ziprasidone 10-20 mg IM for undifferentiated acute psychosis.
  • Lorazepam 1-2 mg IV/IM +/- haloperidol 5 mg IV/IM for rapid tranquilization for safety.
  • Psych ER visits rose from 5% to 12% in early 2000s; inpatient psych beds dropped 62%.

Attending Pearls

  • Just because you're psychotic doesn't mean you don't have a medical problem; be more vigilant, not less.
  • The path to hell is paved with urine drug screens.
  • Medical clearance is a vague, weird term; chart no acute medical problem causing symptoms.
  • There is no such thing as routine tests for medical clearance; base testing on history and physical.
  • Pick out a few mental status exam elements and put them early in the chart; that counts.

Pitfalls

  • Waxing and waning mental status is not expected in primary acute psychosis; suspect delirium or underlying medical cause.
  • Do not dismiss psychotic symptoms as purely psychiatric; patients have elevated risk of concomitant medical problems.
  • Avoid routine urine drug screens; false positives/negatives and self-medication confuse interpretation.
  • A psychiatric hold allows holding only, not treatment against the patient's will.
  • Do not assume anxiety is the diagnosis; it is one of exclusion because symptoms mimic life-threatening conditions.
