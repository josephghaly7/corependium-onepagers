Critical Care Mailbag: Labor of Blunt Trauma Part 1

Trauma · Week 15 · 18 min
Bottom line: Trauma team leadership: dual leadership, bleeding control, targeted resuscitation, access choices.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1If only physician, manage airway from head; otherwise lead from foot of bed with nurse leader.
↓
2Run pre-brief; podium nurse announces time every 5 minutes, handles all requests, assigns tasks.
↓
3Perform primary survey while tracking monitoring, access, find shock, sedation/BP, situational control, initial labs.
↓
4Assume bleeding until proven otherwise; check five sites; if none, consider neurogenic/medical.
↓
5Place nasal capnography on sick trauma; ETCO2 12-14 suggests shock, not hyperventilation.
↓
6Control external bleeding: tourniquet for mangled extremity, foley in hole, whip stitch scalp.
↓
7If suspected hemorrhagic shock, activate massive transfusion protocol and target MAP 65 with blood products.
↓
8Resuscitate to low pressure but high flow; avoid shutting off peripheral perfusion to limit coagulopathy.
↓
9Choose access: two 18G IVs if stable; HD catheter if sick; IO as last ditch.
↓
10If vitals drop, stop task focus; reassess for missed bleeding or need for resuscitative thoracotomy.

📝 Key Facts

  • Five sites of blood loss: environment, intraperitoneal, retroperitoneal, chest, femur.
  • ETCO2 of 12-14 in a breathing trauma patient indicates shock, not hyperventilation.
  • HD catheter ~400 mL/min flow vs Cordis 125-300 mL/min; two large ports.
  • Target MAP 65 for most trauma patients; may move toward MAP 50.
  • Tourniquets now first-line for mangled extremity; previously considered anathema.
  • Podium nurse announces time every 5 minutes and is sole source for all requests.

Attending Pearls

  • Lead from foot of bed with nurse leader beside you for true dual leadership.
  • It's always bleeding until it ain't—make sure bleeding is excluded before other causes.
  • Low pressure but high flow resuscitation preserves tissue perfusion and mitigates trauma coagulopathy.
  • Podium nurse can take over team leadership while physician performs procedures; use nasal capnography on all sick traumas.

Pitfalls

  • Don't rely on ATLS as complete framework; it's a hindrance in experienced trauma centers.
  • Don't place 14G IVs; a failed 14 on the only vessel is worse than two 18s.
  • Don't dismiss low ETCO2 as hyperventilation; assume shock until proven otherwise.
  • Don't let team leader become task-focused; podium nurse must speak up if vitals drop.
