📝 Key Facts

  • AKI definition: increase in creatinine >50% of baseline within 7 days, or ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, or >25% decrease in estimated GFR.
  • AEIOU dialysis indications: Acidemia pH<7.1 despite medical management, Electrolyte (hyperkalemia despite management), Ingestion (lithium/toxic alcohols), Overload (refractory volume overload/pulmonary edema), Uremia (pericarditis, bleeding, encephalopathy).
  • Calcium effect lasts 30-60 minutes; insulin effect lasts about 4 hours.
  • Bicarbonate generally for acidemia pH ≤7.2; infusion: 150 mEq in 1000 mL D5W at 10-25 mEq/hour (100-250 mL/hour).
  • In absence of acidosis, sodium bicarbonate has no benefit.
  • Furosemide IV is twice as potent as oral: 20 mg oral furosemide = 10 mg IV.
  • For patients on oral furosemide with fluid overload: give equivalent of twice total daily oral dose IV bolus (eg, 20 mg oral daily → 20 mg IV).
  • Sodium polystyrene sulfonate (Kayexalate) no longer recommended due to variable efficacy and risk of bowel necrosis; sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (Lokelma) 10,000 mg PO once alternative.

Attending Pearls

  • Use AEIOU to remember dialysis indications and guide temporizing management.
  • Bedside ultrasound is your friend: look at IVC, pericardium, and bladder for retention.
  • Phlebotomy is a back-pocket temporizing measure for pulmonary edema when dialysis delayed; 80% improved markedly in 1995 study.
  • Calcium effect lasts 30-60 min, insulin ~4 hours; redose as needed.
  • Boring is good: stable transfer and eventual dialysis is the goal.

Pitfalls

  • Forgetting calcium's effect lasts only 30-60 minutes and insulin's ~4 hours; potassium may rebound during transfer.
  • Failing to monitor glucose during transport after insulin/D50.
  • Giving bicarbonate to volume-overloaded patient; can worsen volume status due to fluid and salt load.
  • Missing uremic pericarditis/tamponade before transfer; always ultrasound pericardium.
  • Using succinylcholine in hyperkalemia known or strongly suspected.
