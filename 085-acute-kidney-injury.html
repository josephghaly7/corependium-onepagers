085

Acute Kidney Injury

Renal and Urogenital · Week 40 · 12 min
Bottom line: Temporize dialysis-needing patients with hyperkalemia, volume, acidosis, uremia management during transfer.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Identify need for dialysis using AEIOU: acidemia pH<7.1, electrolytes, ingestions, overload, uremia.
↓
2Treat hyperkalemia with calcium, insulin/D50, albuterol; monitor EKG for QRS widening.
↓
3Repeat potassium about 1 hour before departure; redose calcium/insulin if K creeps up.
↓
4Assess volume status with exam, IVC ultrasound, urine output; give small boluses if depleted.
↓
5If volume overloaded, give IV furosemide; monitor for pulmonary edema.
↓
6If pulmonary edema develops, use BiPAP, nitro infusion, or phlebotomy 400-500 mL.
↓
7For severe acidosis pH<7.1 with cardiovascular compromise, consider bicarbonate infusion after nephrology discussion.
↓
8Evaluate for uremic pericarditis with ultrasound; check for pericardial effusion/tamponade.
↓
9Check bladder for urinary retention with bedside ultrasound.
↓
10Arrange transfer; continue albuterol nebs and glucose monitoring during transport.

📝 Key Facts

  • AEIOU dialysis indications: acidemia pH<7.1, hyperkalemia despite management, ingestions, overload, uremia.
  • Calcium effect lasts 30-60 minutes; insulin effect lasts about 4 hours.
  • Repeat potassium 1 hour before scheduled departure to allow time to treat.
  • Phlebotomy: 16-18 gauge, 400-500 mL by gravity over 5-10 minutes; max 500 mL.
  • 1995 study: 80% of pulmonary edema patients improved after phlebotomy; 40% deferred dialysis ~10 hours.
  • Bicarbonate indicated for pH<7.1 with cardiovascular compromise; avoid in volume overload.

Attending Pearls

  • Use AEIOU to remember dialysis indications and guide temporizing management.
  • Bedside ultrasound is your friend: check IVC, pericardium, and bladder.
  • Phlebotomy is a back-pocket temporizing measure for pulmonary edema when dialysis delayed.
  • Boring is good: stable transfer and eventual dialysis is the goal.

Pitfalls

  • Forgetting calcium and insulin effects wear off, allowing potassium to rebound during transport.
  • Failing to monitor glucose during transport after insulin/D50.
  • Giving bicarbonate to volume-overloaded patient, worsening fluid overload.
  • Missing uremic pericarditis or tamponade before transfer; always ultrasound pericardium.
