Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions

Dermatology · Week 50 · 12 min
Bottom line: Severe cutaneous drug reactions: stop offending agent, distinguish SJS/TEN vs DRESS, admit.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Ask about medications started 1-3 weeks ago for SJS/TEN or 2-6 weeks ago for DRESS.
↓
2Examine skin and all mucous membranes including oral, ocular, and anogenital areas.
↓
3Check for Nikolsky sign and Asboe-Hansen sign to support SJS/TEN.
↓
4Classify SJS/TEN by BSA: <10% SJS, 10-30% overlap, >30% TEN.
↓
5For DRESS, assess fever, lymphadenopathy, facial edema, pruritic rash, and abnormal labs.
↓
6Stop the suspected offending drug immediately in all cases.
↓
7Provide pain control for SJS/TEN and manage extensive skin involvement like a burn.
↓
8Obtain early ophthalmology consultation for ocular involvement and keep eyes lubricated.
↓
9Admit severe SJS/TEN to ICU or burn-capable unit.
↓
10Admit DRESS to monitored setting; consider steroids, IVIG, or plasmapheresis.

📝 Key Facts

  • SJS/TEN rash appears 1-3 weeks after drug initiation; DRESS appears 2-6 weeks.
  • SJS is <10% BSA, SJS/TEN overlap 10-30%, TEN >30% BSA.
  • DRESS mortality is 10%; can progress to liver failure, renal failure, CNS or cardiac involvement.
  • Half of pediatric DRESS cases are caused by anticonvulsants: lamotrigine, phenobarb, phenytoin, carbamazepine.
  • Major SJS/TEN drug triggers include allopurinol, anticonvulsants, antibiotics, and NSAIDs.
  • Nikolsky sign: stroking skin causes epidermal separation; Asboe-Hansen sign: pressure on bulla extends it.

Attending Pearls

  • DRESS mental picture: stopped anticonvulsant, seizing and itching child with big red swollen face.
  • DRESS has pronounced facial edema and much less mucosal involvement than SJS/TEN.
  • SJS/TEN often has ocular involvement; early ophthalmology consult is useful but not necessarily urgent overnight.
  • DRESS resolves over weeks and can flare as steroids are weaned.

Pitfalls

  • Failing to ask about medications taken weeks ago, like sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim or NSAIDs.
  • Not examining anogenital mucosa for involvement in suspected SJS/TEN.
  • Underestimating severity because the child seems comfortable; SJS/TEN rash is painful.
  • Assuming DRESS always has eosinophilia; CBC may show other abnormalities, plus elevated LFTs/creatinine.
