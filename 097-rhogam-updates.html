RhoGAM Updates

OB/GYN · Week 42 · src: Rhogam immune globulin, Early Pregnancy Loss
Bottom line: No routine RHIG under 12 weeks miscarriage/abortion; still for ectopic, molar, and ≥12 weeks.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Order RH typing while awaiting ultrasound gestational age if RH status unknown.
↓
2If <12 weeks with miscarriage/abortion (expectant, medication, or procedural), defer routine RH testing and RHIG.
↓
3If patient requests RHIG or shared decision-making favors it, consider giving on individual basis.
↓
4If ≥12 weeks with miscarriage/abortion, administer RHIG.
↓
5If ectopic or molar pregnancy at any gestational age, administer RHIG.
↓
6If abdominal trauma in pregnancy, follow unchanged RHIG recommendations.
↓
7If RHIG indicated and <13 weeks gestation, administer 50 μg IM as soon as possible and within 72 hours.
↓
8If RHIG indicated and ≥13 weeks gestation, administer 300 μg IM as soon as possible and within 72 hours.
↓
9If 300 μg more readily available or gestational age unsure, 300 μg IM is safe to give.
↓
10When uncertain or dates unclear, consult OB-GYN to confirm management.

📝 Key Facts

  • About 15% of women in the United States are RH negative.
  • FDA approved RH immunoglobulin in the 1960s; at delivery sensitization dropped from ~10% to 1-2%.
  • Adding a 28-week dose further reduced sensitization to 0.1-0.2%.
  • 2020 University of Pennsylvania and 2023 multicenter cohort studies found first-trimester fetal RBC exposure below sensitization threshold.
  • ACOG and French College in 2024 recommend foregoing routine RH testing and RHIG for <12-week miscarriage/abortion.
  • WHO, Society for Family Planning (2022), and RCOG UK issued similar recommendations earlier.
  • RHIG still indicated for loss/abortion at ≥12 weeks and for ectopic and molar pregnancies at all gestational ages.
  • No changes in RHIG indications for routine pregnancies, abdominal trauma, or RH-negative mothers without complications.

Attending Pearls

  • ACOG aligned with other international societies; this is not coming out of left field.
  • Overuse in developed countries contributes to global and national shortages, limiting access where RHIG has proven benefit.
  • If dates are uncertain, order RH typing while determining gestational age; 72-hour window allows deferral if questions remain.
  • RHIG is safe; can consider on individual basis with shared decision-making if patient requests.
  • If unsure, talk to your OB-GYN consultant to confirm.

Pitfalls

  • Routinely giving RHIG to all RH-negative first-trimester miscarriage/abortion patients despite no proven benefit.
  • Forgetting RHIG is still indicated for ectopic, molar, and ≥12-week pregnancies.
  • Treating RHIG as time-critical; you have 72 hours, so can defer while clarifying dates or questions.
  • Changing ED practice without coordinating with OB-GYN/family medicine, causing inconsistent protocols and patient education.
