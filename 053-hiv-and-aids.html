📝 Key Facts

  • AIDS defined as HIV infection with CD4 <200 cells/µL and/or an AIDS-defining illness.
  • Acute HIV syndrome: mono-like illness with fever, fatigue, sore throat, maculopapular rash, headache, lymphadenopathy, myalgias.
  • Up to half of all HIV transmissions occur during acute HIV illness.
  • p24 antigen positive when viral load reaches ~10,000 copies.
  • Total lymphocyte count <1000 predicts CD4 count <200.
  • CD4 count declines ~80–100 cells/µL per year during latent phase.
  • CD4 >500: typical immunocompetent infections; CD4 200–499: increased OI susceptibility.
  • CXR may be non-diagnostic in patients with CD4 <200 cells/µL.

Attending Pearls

  • HIV is now a chronic manageable disease, not a death sentence, with treatment and access.
  • Early diagnosis and treatment of acute HIV reduces transmission and may improve long-term prognosis.
  • Use total lymphocyte count <1000 as a quick bedside predictor of CD4 <200.
  • HIV management is complex; involve HIV specialists for drug regimens and long-term care.

Pitfalls

  • Do not rely on a negative HIV antibody test during acute infection; seroconversion takes 3–8 weeks.
  • Do not send viral loads willy-nilly in asymptomatic patients; false positives occur.
  • Do not trust a CD4 count older than a few months; assume the worst.
  • Do not dismiss acute HIV as a nonspecific viral syndrome without considering risk factors.
  • Do not expect leukocytosis in HIV sepsis; SIRS criteria are less likely to be present.
