Ectopic Pregnancy Management

OB/GYN · Week 42 · 23 min
Bottom line: Ectopic care: act on high beta, share decisions, ensure follow-up despite disparities.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1In pregnancy with abdominal pain/bleeding, obtain transvaginal ultrasound and beta-HCG to identify IUP, ectopic, or rupture.
↓
2If ruptured ectopic, start resuscitation and call GYN for surgery.
↓
3If unruptured ectopic, consult GYN for methotrexate versus surgery.
↓
4If no IUP and beta-HCG <2000, arrange repeat beta-HCG and ultrasound in 48 hours.
↓
5If no IUP and beta-HCG >4000, treat as non-viable pregnancy; consult GYN for management.
↓
6If no IUP and beta-HCG 2000-4000, engage patient in shared decision-making about options.
↓
7If choosing methotrexate, check renal/liver function, hemoglobin, bleeding history, and contraindications.
↓
8Administer methotrexate in ED in consultation with GYN if candidate.
↓
9After methotrexate, repeat beta-HCG on day 4 and day 7; ensure follow-up to zero.
↓
10If beta-HCG not resolving, arrange GYN evaluation for surgery or second methotrexate dose.

📝 Key Facts

  • Beta-HCG >4000 with no IUP on ultrasound indicates near 100% non-viable pregnancy; treat.
  • Gray zone is beta-HCG 2000-4000: viable pregnancy unlikely but not impossible.
  • Heterotopic pregnancy without assisted reproductive therapy occurs in about 1 in 10,000-11,000.
  • Methotrexate contraindications include renal insufficiency, immunodeficiency, active pulmonary/peptic ulcer disease, hypersensitivity, breastfeeding, heterotopic viable IUP, beta-HCG >5000, fetal cardiac activity.
  • Current ACOG recommendation is not to give Rhogam before 12 weeks; active bleeding cases unclear.
  • IUP with fetal heartbeat and no assisted reproductive therapy functionally rules out ectopic.

Attending Pearls

  • The ectopic algorithm hasn't changed; access to follow-up and legal climate have.
  • In the 2000-4000 gray zone, patient priorities should guide shared decision-making.
  • ED physicians in rural/freestanding settings may need to give methotrexate and manage follow-up without on-site GYN.
  • State-to-state legal differences create a new disparity of care; border EDs see patients seeking options.

Pitfalls

  • Discharging a patient with diagnosed ectopic because follow-up is unavailable risks EMTALA violation.
  • Delaying care until fetal pole with heartbeat is seen can eliminate methotrexate option and force surgery.
  • Assuming reliable 48-hour or day-4/day-7 follow-up exists; in many regions patients return to ED.
  • Automatically giving Rhogam before 12 weeks without checking current ACOG guidance or GYN consult.
