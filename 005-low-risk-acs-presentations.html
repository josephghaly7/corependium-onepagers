Low Risk ACS Presentations

Cardiology · Week 3 · src: Low Risk Chest Pain
Bottom line: Low-risk ACS: only 5-10% have ACS; use HEART pathway, ECG/troponin, discharge ≤3.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Obtain ECG within 10 min of arrival for chest pain; place on cardiac monitor and consider IV access.
↓
2Exclude high-risk features: STEMI, hemodynamic instability, dynamic ECG changes, or unstable angina concern.
↓
3Administer aspirin 162-325 mg chewable non-enteric coated.
↓
4Consider nitroglycerin 0.4 mg SL unless hypotensive, inferior/RV MI, or on phosphodiesterase inhibitors.
↓
5Send troponin; consider CBC, BMP, PT/INR if anticoagulated; d-dimer/BNP if PE or heart failure suspected.
↓
6If concern for dissection, obtain contrast-enhanced chest CT angiogram; bedside echo if tamponade concern.
↓
7Repeat ECG every 15-30 min during first hour if ongoing symptoms; also if symptoms change or resolve.
↓
8Apply HEART pathway: modified HEART score, negative serial troponins, and ECG without ischemia.
↓
9Low score ≤3 with serial troponins: suitable for discharge home with close outpatient follow-up.
↓
10Moderate score 4-6: admit, observe, or expedited outpatient evaluation; high score ≥7: admit for cardiac evaluation.

📝 Key Facts

  • Less than 10% (podcast: 5-10%) of ED chest pain patients are ultimately diagnosed with ACS.
  • Missed MI remains one of the most common reasons for ED litigation in the United States.
  • Big three can't-miss causes: ACS, PE, thoracic aortic dissection.
  • Additional life-threatening causes: pericarditis/pericardial tamponade, tension pneumothorax, esophageal rupture.
  • MACE defined as: AMI, PCI, CABG, coronary angiography revealing procedurally correctable stenosis managed conservatively, and death due to any cause.
  • HEART score components: History, ECG, Age, Risk factors, Troponin.
  • HEART score/pathway should not be used in STEMI, acute ischemic ECG changes, or concern for unstable angina.
  • Very low risk patients who may not need workup: momentary transient pain lasting a second, pain lasting over 8 hours with normal ECG, or single-point reproducible pain.

Attending Pearls

  • The goal for ECG acquisition should be within 10 min of arrival when patients present with chest pain or other symptoms concerning for ACS.
  • Consider the "big three" causes of life-threatening chest pain in your initial differential.
  • The ECG is the cornerstone of the diagnosis of ACS; compare current ECGs to previous ECGs to avoid attributing old findings to current ischemia or failing to notice new changes.
  • If concerned about an ECG, say 'let's repeat this in 10 minutes'—decisive and catches evolving changes.
  • After a negative ED evaluation, shared decision-making can facilitate provider-patient conversations regarding appropriate next steps with physician consideration of risk post ED evaluation, patient availability and motivation for follow up, and patient comfort level with expedited outpatient evaluation.

Pitfalls

  • Women, the elderly, and patients with diabetes often present with atypical symptoms and anginal equivalents in lieu of chest pain.
  • Avoid prematurely settling on a psychiatric diagnosis; anxiety is a common associated symptom in ACS, PE, and thoracic aortic dissection.
  • Order a d-dimer in low to moderate risk PE patients; do not order in "no risk" patients due to high false positive rate.
  • Do not use HEART score or pathway in patients with STEMI, acute ischemic ECG changes, or concern for unstable angina.
  • Being falsely reassured by a normal-appearing ECG; everyone's ECG is normal until it isn't.
