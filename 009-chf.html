CHF

Cardiology · Week 5 · src: Heart Failure, Pediatric Heart Failure
Bottom line: CHF: 1 million new US diagnoses/year; classify by BP and treat preload, afterload, contractility, rate, rhythm.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1In known CHF, ask: new murmur (surgery?), new renal failure, ischemia, arrhythmia needing immediate treatment.
↓
2Classify by BP: high (SCAPE/hypertensive), low (cardiogenic shock), or normal.
↓
3Pulmonary edema + high BP: start CPAP 5 cm H2O, titrate up to 15 cm H2O, titrate to work of breathing.
↓
4BiPAP if concomitant COPD and ventilation required; use POCUS for B lines, IVC, EPSS/EF.
↓
5Give IV nitroglycerin 100-200 µg/min, titrate every 2-5 min (max 400 µg/min); 80 µg/min equals SL NTG.
↓
6Diurese within first 90 min if indicated: furosemide 40-80 mg IV push (or equivalent loop diuretic).
↓
7Pulmonary edema + low BP: small fluid challenges 200-250 mL isotonic crystalloid only if hypovolemic; do not give if volume overloaded.
↓
8Start norepinephrine first for cardiogenic shock: 0.05-0.5 µg/kg/min (alt 0.5-50 µg/min), titrate every 2-5 min to MAP >=65.
↓
9Add dobutamine or milrinone only after MAP goal achieved; early cardiology/CT surgery consult for mechanical support.
↓
10Address rate/rhythm: amiodarone preferred in HFrEF; avoid diltiazem if EF severely reduced.

📝 Key Facts

  • 1 million new CHF diagnoses/year: 70% worsening known CHF, 15% first diagnosis, 5% end-stage.
  • Framingham major criteria (2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor = CHF): PND/orthopnea, JVD, rales, cardiomegaly, acute pulmonary edema, S3, venous pressure >16 cm H2O, circulation time >25 sec, hepatojugular reflex.
  • Framingham minor criteria: ankle edema, night cough, dyspnea on exertion, hepatomegaly, pleural effusion, vital capacity reduced by one third, tachycardia >120.
  • Cardiomegaly on CXR ~75% sensitive for CHF; S3 very specific; most other findings <60%.
  • Positive hepatojugular reflex: JVP rises >3 cm after 10 sec (up to 1 min) of RUQ pressure.
  • CHF mortality 18-20% per year; five-year survival terrible; worse than most cancers.
  • EF below 60 considered abnormal; normal ~60-65%, systolic failure often 10-30%.
  • Pediatric CHF most commonly caused by congenital heart disease; consider VSD (weeks-months), myocarditis, SVT.

Attending Pearls

  • Pump no worky: bottom line for CHF.
  • Most common cause of right-sided heart failure is left-sided heart failure.
  • There is no gold standard for CHF diagnosis; use global assessment.
  • When unsure between COPD and CHF exacerbation, treat for both while working it out.
  • Ask: new murmur (surgery), new renal failure, ischemia, arrhythmia.

Pitfalls

  • Do not give fluids in cardiogenic shock if volume overloaded; use only 200-250 mL challenges if hypovolemic.
  • Do not use diltiazem in severely reduced EF; it is a negative inotrope and can cause decompensation.
  • Do not start dobutamine or milrinone before achieving MAP goal with norepinephrine.
  • Do not hold diuretics for hypotension or AKI.
  • Do not rely on normal CXR heart size to exclude CHF, especially with COPD; cardiomegaly only ~75% sensitive.
