Altered Mental Status: Management Discussions

Neurology · Week 19 · src: Approach to Altered Mental Status
Bottom line: Altered mental status requires immediate ABCs, glucose, C-spine, and broad work-up; correct sodium no faster than 0.5 mEq/hr (10-12/day).

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Place on cardiac monitor and continuous pulse oximetry; secure IV access; obtain point-of-care glucose.
↓
2Maintain cervical spine precautions; assume trauma unless clear history otherwise.
↓
3Treat hypoglycemia (glucose <55 mg/dL): dextrose 50% 12.5-50 g IV push, repeat if needed; glucagon 1 mg IM if no IV.
↓
4For opioid overdose: naloxone 0.4-2 mg IV/IO; higher doses (10 mg) may be needed for fentanyl/methadone.
↓
5If unable to protect airway or deteriorating, prepare for RSI; use rocuronium if hyperkalemia likely (ESRD, heat stroke, rhabdomyolysis).
↓
6Consider hypercarbia: obtain ABG/VBG; treat with NIPPV or intubation; maintain SpO2 >90%.
↓
7Check for focal neurologic findings, especially pupillary exam; obtain non-contrast head CT if unknown etiology.
↓
8If non-convulsive status epilepticus suspected: obtain EEG if possible; immediate Neurology consult; consider benzodiazepines, then phenytoin/fosphenytoin, valproic acid, or levetiracetam.
↓
9Check core temperature: if <35°C, warm with forced air convection and warm IV fluids; if >40°C, cool with evaporative cooling, ice packs, or cold saline bladder irrigation.
↓
10If bacterial meningitis suspected: give dexamethasone and antibiotics before CT/LP; LP contraindicated if platelets <75, INR >1.5, clinical bleeding, or signs of herniation (blown pupil, asymmetric pupils, sudden paralysis).

📝 Key Facts

  • Hypoglycemia is defined as glucose <55 mg/dL (<3.1 mmol/L) for treatment of altered mental status.
  • Hyponatremia: severe symptoms (coma, seizures) usually occur below 120 mEq/L; levels <120 warrant monitored bed.
  • Hypernatremia: >155 mEq/L is concerning and warrants monitored bed due to seizure risk.
  • Hypercalcemia: >14 mg/dL (3.5 mmol/L) can cause altered mental status; treatment is IV fluids, calcitonin, bisphosphonates.
  • Hypothermia: core temperature <35°C requires warming measures; hyperthermia >40°C requires aggressive cooling.
  • LP contraindications: platelets <75,000, INR >1.5, clinical bleeding, or signs of herniation (blown pupil, asymmetric pupils, sudden paralysis).
  • Myxedema coma mortality up to 40%; give IV thyroxine, IV cortisone, supportive care, and treat underlying infection.
  • Serotonin syndrome and neuroleptic malignant syndrome are rare but should be considered after common causes ruled out; both require ICU care and specific treatments.

Attending Pearls

  • Just because they have alcohol withdrawal doesn't mean they don't have a brain hemorrhage, meningitis, or hypoglycemia.
  • If you change osmolarity really quickly, you run the risk of having someone's neurons explode; never exceed 10-12 mEq/day sodium correction.
  • A seizing hyponatremic patient: 3% saline 100 cc over 10 min, then 100 cc over 50 min, then stop.
  • Mixing edema coma is an endocrine emergency with mortality up to 40%.
  • If they're still confused after all this, then we're confused too.

Pitfalls

  • Assuming alcohol withdrawal without ruling out subdural hematoma, meningitis, or hypoglycemia.
  • Correcting sodium too rapidly: >0.5 mEq/hr or >10-12 mEq/day causes central pontine myelinolysis (ODS).
  • Missing chronic salicylate poisoning; often misdiagnosed as sepsis; requires dialysis.
  • Giving physostigmine for anticholinergic toxicity: risk of dysrhythmia; conservative management preferred.
  • Performing LP in patients with coagulopathy (platelets <75, INR >1.5) or signs of herniation.
