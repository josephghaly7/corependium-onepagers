Altered Mental Status: Management Discussions

Neurology · Week 19 · 57 min
Bottom line: Altered mental status: stabilize, treat reversible causes, CT, then consider exotic causes.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Start IV/O2 monitor; give oxygen if hypoxic; protect C-spine; check glucose early.
↓
2If glucose <60, give ampule glucose; if opioid signs, give naloxone.
↓
3Get urine pregnancy, hemoglobin, EKG; send acetaminophen, salicylate, LFTs, TSH, electrolytes.
↓
4If fever, consider infection/meningitis; if clinically meningitis, give empiric antibiotics and steroids before CT.
↓
5If unknown and initial survey negative, order non-contrast head CT.
↓
6Hold LP if platelets <75, INR >1.5, clinical bleeding, or herniation signs.
↓
7If alcohol withdrawal suspected, treat with benzodiazepines; still rule out bleed, meningitis, hypoglycemia.
↓
8If dialysis patient, get EKG for hyperkalemia; if uremic encephalopathy likely, arrange dialysis.
↓
9For sodium: stable NS 100-200 mL/hr; seizing 3% saline 100 mL/10 min then 100 mL/50 min.
↓
10If hepatic encephalopathy, give lactulose; if hypertensive encephalopathy no focal deficit, lower BP 20-30%.

📝 Key Facts

  • Non-contrast head CT is initial imaging for unknown altered mental status; contrast CT rarely needed initially.
  • LP contraindications: platelets <75, INR >1.5, clinical bleeding/DIC, or herniation signs (asymmetric pupils, falling GCS).
  • Never correct serum sodium faster than 0.5 mEq/hour or 10-12 mEq/day; rapid correction causes central pontine myelinolysis/ODS.
  • Seizing severe hyponatremia (sodium ~100-110): 3% saline 100 mL over 10 min, then 100 mL over 50 min; raises sodium 3-6; stop.
  • Myxedema coma mortality up to 40%; presents with hypothermia, bradycardia, waxy skin, slow reflexes, non-pitting edema.
  • Thyroid storm mimics sepsis/CHF; treatment includes IV beta blockers, PTU/methimazole, steroids, potassium iodide. Hypercalcemia >14 mg/dL can cause altered mental status.

Attending Pearls

  • Just because a patient has alcohol withdrawal doesn't mean they don't also have a brain bleed, meningitis, or low glucose.
  • Patients didn't get to sodium 107 overnight; take your time correcting.
  • An intern who knows what they don't know kills fewer patients than one who thinks they know it all.
  • Supportive care: whatever they can't do themselves, you do for them; for tox, call Poison Center but know basics.

Pitfalls

  • Assuming alcohol withdrawal without ruling out subdural hematoma, meningitis, or hypoglycemia.
  • Correcting sodium too quickly—most common error—causes central pontine myelinolysis/ODS.
  • Missing myxedema coma, thyroid storm, or salicylate poisoning because they mimic sepsis/CHF.
  • Lowering blood pressure in a possible stroke with focal deficit unless giving tPA.
