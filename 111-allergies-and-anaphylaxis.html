Allergies and Anaphylaxis

Hematology and Oncology · Week 48 · src: Allergy, Hypersensitivity, and Anaphylaxis
Bottom line: Anaphylaxis: give IM epinephrine 0.5 mg early, treat ABC, fluids; >2-3 doses start infusion.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Manage airway first: intubate if progressive airway edema, respiratory failure, or inspiratory stridor.
↓
2Consider awake fiberoptic intubation as a mitigation strategy for airway compromise.
↓
3Maximize first pass intubation success; prepare for difficult airway and possible cricothyroidotomy/surgical airway.
↓
4Administer inhaled bronchodilators for wheezing.
↓
5Give IM epinephrine early into the anterolateral thigh; repeat every 5 minutes as needed.
↓
6If rapidly decompensating, use 0.3-mg auto-injector; second dose to total 0.5 mg.
↓
7If >2-3 IM epinephrine doses required, start IV epinephrine infusion 0.01-0.1 mcg/kg/min, titrate by 0.01-0.05 mcg/kg/min every 2-3 minutes.
↓
8Give IV isotonic crystalloid 0.9% NaCl or Ringer lactate 20-30 mL/kg bolus.
↓
9For refractory hypotension on beta blockers, give glucagon 1-5 mg IV then infusion 5-15 mcg/min.
↓
10Consider ECMO if refractory to these treatments; adjuncts bronchodilators, antihistamines, corticosteroids.

📝 Key Facts

  • Anaphylaxis criteria 1: acute mucocutaneous symptoms plus respiratory symptoms OR hypotension/end-organ damage.
  • Anaphylaxis criteria 2: two or more of mucocutaneous, respiratory, hypotension/end-organ hypoperfusion, or GI symptoms rapidly after known/likely allergen.
  • Anaphylaxis criteria 3: hypotension within minutes to several hours after exposure to a known allergen.
  • Up to 20% of anaphylaxis patients have no mucocutaneous symptoms, contributing to under-recognition.
  • Hypotension within minutes to several hours after a known allergen is sufficient for anaphylaxis.
  • Epinephrine is first-line treatment for suspected anaphylaxis; adjuncts are IV fluids, bronchodilators, antihistamines, corticosteroids.
  • Epinephrine comes in 2 concentrations: 1 mg/mL and 0.1 mg/mL.
  • Ratio dosing (eg, 1:1,000) should not be used because of potential for dosing errors.

Attending Pearls

  • If A, B, or C, do E: give epinephrine for any airway, breathing, or circulation issue.
  • Epinephrine is the one life-saving medication and intervention besides securing the airway.
  • When in doubt, give epinephrine; no absolute contraindications, even with heart disease.
  • Patients on beta blockers with refractory anaphylaxis should be given glucagon.
  • Patients with cardiopulmonary failure or airway compromise may require vasopressors, mechanical ventilation, and other critical interventions.

Pitfalls

  • Under-recognition and undertreatment of anaphylaxis.
  • Missing anaphylaxis in the up to 20% without mucocutaneous symptoms.
  • Using ratio dosing (eg, 1:1,000) for epinephrine, risking dosing errors.
  • Pediatric epinephrine IV bolus dosing carries high potential for 10-fold dosing errors; use infusion or repeated IM instead.
  • Errors related to epinephrine concentration and dilution can cause serious adverse events.
