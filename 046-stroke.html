Stroke

Neurology · Week 17 · src: Pediatric Stroke, Acute Ischemic Stroke
Bottom line: Ischemic stroke: thrombolysis <4.5h, BP <185/110 pre-TPA, CT first branch point.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess ABCs; intubate if airway compromised; O2 if sat <94% (peds >95%).
↓
2Establish time of symptom onset as accurately as possible.
↓
3Rapid neuro exam or NIH stroke scale; check fingerstick glucose.
↓
4Check BP; if TPA candidate keep <185/110; pre-TPA stroke/TIA <220/120.
↓
5Obtain non-contrast head CT immediately with minimal pre-CT delays.
↓
6If CT negative/ischemic and onset <4.5h with disabling deficits, give alteplase or tenecteplase.
↓
7Consult neurology/neurosurgery for reperfusion; endovascular therapy up to 24h for LVO.
↓
8Position head midline neutral, supine as tolerated; pediatric: flat if alert, HOB 30 if not alert/vomiting.
↓
9Post-CT labs: CBC, chemistry, coags, troponin; ECG and chest x-ray.
↓
10If not straightforward, consider mimics (dissection, sepsis, pneumonia).

📝 Key Facts

  • TPA window: NINDS 3 hours; current guidelines up to 4.5 hours.
  • BP targets: ischemic stroke/TIA <220/120 mm Hg; with TPA <185/110 mm Hg.
  • CT is first major branch point: hemorrhagic vs ischemic vs mimic.
  • Up to 1 in 5 stroke patients have elevated troponin; associated with higher mortality.
  • Complete occlusion; CTA head/neck identifies LVO for endovascular therapy.
  • MRI with diffusion-weighted sequencing is gold standard for acute ischemic stroke but can be falsely negative early.
  • Pediatric strokes: delay in diagnosis more common; greater proportion hemorrhagic than adults.
  • Non-contrast head CT identifies hemorrhage or contraindications to thrombolysis.

Attending Pearls

  • Time is brain; get to CT as quickly as possible, limit pre-CT actions to essentials.
  • Do tasks in parallel: start mixing TPA as soon as negative head CT while completing workup.
  • Remember heart-brain relationship; consider cardiac causes and aortic dissection.
  • Don't pigeonhole as stroke; keep broad differential for mimics like sepsis, dissection, pneumonia.
  • Delay in diagnosis occurs more frequently among pediatric patients.

Pitfalls

  • Delaying CT to obtain detailed history, EKG, or chest x-ray before scanning.
  • Giving TPA to a patient with hypoglycemia; always check glucose first.
  • Pigeonholing as stroke and missing mimics like aortic dissection, sepsis, or pneumonia.
  • Forgetting airway protection before CT in a stroke patient who can't protect airway.
  • Major risk of reperfusion therapy is symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage; monitor closely for deterioration.
