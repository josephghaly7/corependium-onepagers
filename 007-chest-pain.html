Chest Pain

Cardiology · Week 4 · src: Approach to Chest Pain, Low Risk Chest Pain
Bottom line: Chest pain: ECG in 10 min, HEART pathway-rule out STEMI, PE, dissection; <=3 discharge.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1ECG within 10 min of arrival for chest pain or ACS symptoms; monitor and IV access.
↓
2If unstable, simultaneous assessment and call for help.
↓
3For hypotension: anterior MI vasopressors; inferior MI IV fluids; bradycardia atropine.
↓
4Repeat ECG every 15-30 min first hour if ongoing symptoms; also if symptoms change or resolve.
↓
5STEMI: activate cath lab or thrombolytics; consider bedside echo to aid cath decision.
↓
6PE: assess pretest probability; high risk straight to CTPA; low risk D-dimer <500 rules out.
↓
7PE with shock: consider empiric TPA without confirmatory test.
↓
8Aortic dissection: control HR <60 then SBP <120; pain control with titratable fentanyl.
↓
9Dissection: esmolol first, then add vasodilator; titrate to pain and higher arm BP.
↓
10Low-risk HEART score <=3 with serial troponins: discharge with close follow-up.

📝 Key Facts

  • ECG acquisition goal within 10 min of arrival for chest pain or ACS symptoms.
  • HEART score components: History, ECG, Age, Risk factors, Troponin.
  • HEART score low <=3, moderate 4-6, high >=7.
  • HEART score/pathway not for STEMI, acute ischemic ECG changes, or concern for unstable angina.
  • MACE defined as AMI, PCI, CABG, angiography with correctable stenosis managed conservatively, death.
  • Chest pain >8 million US ED visits/year; second most common chief complaint.
  • ACS >1 million cases/year; PE hundreds of thousands; dissection thousands.
  • About half of PE patients have no identifiable risk factors.

Attending Pearls

  • Use Bayesian thinking: interpret every test based on patient's pretest probability.
  • Risk factors only get you so far; trust the story in front of you.
  • In dissection, pain relief indicates treatment success; titrate therapy to pain and higher arm BP.
  • If dissection suspected and ECG shows STEMI, dissection likely caused it; involve cardiology and surgery.
  • The HEART score is easy to remember: History, ECG, Age, Risk factors, Troponin.

Pitfalls

  • Prematurely settling on psychiatric diagnosis; anxiety is common in ACS, PE, and dissection.
  • Women, elderly, and diabetics often present atypically with anginal equivalents.
  • Ordering D-dimer in no-risk patients yields high false positive rate.
  • Using HEART score in hemodynamic instability, dynamic ECG changes, or significant unstable angina concern.
  • Giving nitrates in inferior/RV MI, hypotension, bradycardia, tachycardia, or PDE inhibitor use.
