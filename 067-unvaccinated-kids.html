067

Unvaccinated Kids

Pediatrics · Week 25 · 15 min
Bottom line: Well-appearing febrile unvaccinated children generally need no different testing; close follow-up advised.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess for ill appearance; if ill-appearing, pursue standard sepsis evaluation and management.
↓
2If well-appearing febrile child with incomplete/absent vaccinations, check community vaccination rates and recent outbreaks.
↓
3If from low-vaccination community or outbreak, consider blood culture, CBC, ceftriaxone, and close follow-up.
↓
4If not high-risk, treat like vaccinated child; do not obtain routine labs solely for immunization status.
↓
5If CBC obtained and WBC >15,000, draw blood cultures and give ceftriaxone.
↓
6If WBC >15,000, have patient return next day for culture check and repeat ceftriaxone.
↓
7Arrange follow-up with pediatrician within 24 hours; if unavailable, advise return to ED.
↓
8If blood culture obtained, call family when positive and use opportunity to discuss vaccination.

📝 Key Facts

  • Current occult bacteremia rate is <0.5%; cost-effectiveness analysis suggests testing is not reasonable below 1%.
  • 95% of positive blood cultures in well-appearing febrile children are negative on repeat culture, even without antibiotics.
  • 2021 Dunnets study: 4,742 encounters age 2-36 months; bacteremia 1.5% overall, 1.3% in un/under-vaccinated; no significant difference.
  • In Dunnets study, all 5 Strep pneumoniae bacteremias occurred in fully vaccinated children; Staph aureus and E. coli were most common.
  • Full vaccine protection likely after 3 sets (around 6 months); not before 4 months after two series.
  • CBC >15,000 may help detect Strep pneumoniae, but Hib and Neisseria often do not elevate WBC.

Attending Pearls

  • Neonates are managed differently due to immunologic immaturity, not vaccination status.
  • Consider increased caution for children from low-vaccination communities or recent outbreaks.
  • Clinical judgment and close follow-up within 24 hours are key, regardless of vaccination status.
  • Even fully vaccinated children can develop invasive bacterial disease; stay vigilant.

Pitfalls

  • Routine blood cultures in unvaccinated well-appearing children cause contamination more often than detecting true bacteremia.
  • Relying on CBC as a risk-stratification tool is outdated and can miss Hib and Neisseria.
  • Treating all unvaccinated children as ticking time bombs ignores evidence and exposes them to unnecessary antibiotics.
  • Letting rare anecdotal bad outcomes drive management of the entire population ignores intervention risks.
