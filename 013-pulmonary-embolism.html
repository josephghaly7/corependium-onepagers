Pulmonary Embolism

Cardiology · Week 7 · src: Pulmonary Embolism, General Approach to Pediatric History and PE
Bottom line: Risk stratify first; high-risk PE = hypotension >15 min + RV dysfunction; give heparin then lytics.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Risk stratify clinically first (gestalt + PERC/Wells/Revised Geneva/YEARS) to set pretest probability.
↓
2Low risk (<20%): PE rule-out criteria (PERC) or D-dimer.
↓
3Moderate risk (21%-50%): D-dimer, then CTPA if positive.
↓
4High risk (>50%): empiric heparin + CTPA; may need additional testing.
↓
5Begin anticoagulation as soon as PE confirmed unless contraindicated; IV unfractionated heparin if lysis/procedure planned.
↓
6High-risk PE: systemic thrombolysis (first-line if catheter intervention delayed/unavailable) vs CDL/PMT per institution.
↓
7Contraindication to lysis: catheter-directed therapy/PMT (if intermediate-high risk or high risk) or open surgical thrombectomy; call cardiothoracic surgery early.
↓
8Catastrophic PE (profound shock, high-dose pressors, arrest): VA-ECMO first, before systemic lysis or catheter intervention; ECMO is bridge to definitive therapy.
↓
9Avoid/delay intubation; if needed, 250 mL fluid boluses + norepinephrine first, consider ECMO evaluation.
↓
10Oxygenate: target SpO2 >90%; consider HFNC, nebulized nitroglycerin or nitric oxide for pulmonary vasodilation.

📝 Key Facts

  • Pre-test probability: low <20% → PERC/D-dimer; moderate 21%-50% → D-dimer → CTPA; high >50% → empiric heparin + CTPA.
  • Thrombolytic contraindication must be checked with a standard rapid checklist.
  • Unfractionated heparin: 80-100 units/kg IV bolus (max 10,000 units), then 18 units/kg/hour (max 2,000 units/hour), titrated to anti-Xa 0.3-0.7.
  • If anticoagulation contraindicated, consider IVC filter and start anticoagulation when safe.
  • CDL/PMT may be preferred over systemic lysis in intermediate-high-risk PE at experienced centers.
  • PMT indications: high-risk PE with lytic contraindication or failed systemic lysis; intermediate-high-risk PE with RV dysfunction but no hypotension.
  • Open surgical thrombectomy reserved for absolute lytic contraindication or rescue after failed lysis; requires cardiopulmonary bypass and sternotomy.
  • Risk factors are additive: trauma, immobilization, DVT/superficial vein thrombosis, oral contraceptives, estrogen, pregnancy, obesity, smoking, hypercoagulable states.

Attending Pearls

  • You must first risk stratify the person clinically, and that will define what tests you do and how well that test will perform.
  • Patients die of PE from the acute hemodynamic problem, not the hypoxia.
  • The right ventricle is a skinny bag that cannot handle the abrupt rise in pulmonary pressure.
  • Clot size is less relevant than the physiologic consequences of that clot.
  • In severe PE, the right heart can balloon and bow into the left ventricle, causing LV outflow obstruction.

Pitfalls

  • Intubating or sedating before hemodynamic resuscitation and ECMO evaluation, risking cardiovascular collapse.
  • Giving excessive IV fluids that worsen RV distension and impair cardiac output.
  • Relying on clot size rather than physiologic consequences — segmental PE can be unstable, saddle embolus can be stable.
  • Missing atypical presentations because symptoms may be mild, nonspecific, or absent.
  • Failing to reassess risk category — patients can progress between low, intermediate, high-risk, and catastrophic PE.
