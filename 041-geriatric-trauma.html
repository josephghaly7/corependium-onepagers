Geriatric Trauma

Trauma · Week 16 · src: Dental Trauma, Ear Trauma
Bottom line: Elderly falls are high-risk; scan head injuries liberally and prevent next fall.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Recognize geriatric fall as nonsyncopal fall from standing; avoid labeling as mechanical fall.
↓
2Assess for acute causes: dehydration, electrolytes, infection, dysrhythmias; tailor EKG/labs to history and exam.
↓
3Determine baseline mental status; if reduced GCS from baseline or baseline unknown, obtain head CT.
↓
4Obtain head CT in elderly after ground-level fall, especially with head/facial bruising or laceration, new neurologic deficit, CKD, or reduced GCS.
↓
5Do not rely on anticoagulation status alone to decide scanning; age itself confers high ICH risk.
↓
6Review medication list; every medication over 4 carries 14% increased fall risk; identify new/changed high-risk meds.
↓
7Refer recurrent fallers to primary care, falls clinic, geriatrics clinic, or home visit program for holistic evaluation.
↓
8Consult ED physical therapy to assess mobility and needs; can reduce fall-related revisits by 30% at 30 and 60 days.
↓
9Provide assistive device or outpatient PT referral if physical therapy identifies need.
↓
10Disposition based on injuries, acute causes found, medication issues, and home safety; treat index fall as sentinel event.

📝 Key Facts

  • About one-third of community-dwelling adults 65+ fall yearly; rises to two-thirds in those over 80 or in nursing facilities.
  • Falls are the number one leading cause of trauma-related mortality in older patients, usually from ground-level falls.
  • About 14% of older adult falls result in injury; top injuries are hip/lower extremity, trunk/spine, and skull/intracranial injuries.
  • Older blunt head injury patients have about 5% intracranial hemorrhage rate, with no difference based on anticoagulation.
  • Anticoagulated ground-level fall with head injury: ICH rates up to 15% (aspirin/clopidogrel/warfarin), 12% (clopidogrel), 5% (warfarin), 9% (GCS 15 on warfarin).
  • Canadian Head CT excludes patients over 65; New Orleans head injury tool excludes patients over 60.
  • Four independent ICH predictors: head bruise/laceration, new neurologic exam abnormality, chronic kidney disease, reduced GCS from baseline.
  • ED physical therapy reduces fall-related ED revisits by 30% at 30 and 60 days.

Attending Pearls

  • The best fall is the one that never happens; index fall may be sentinel event to prevent future hip fracture or ICH.
  • Don't feel bad about negative head CTs; ICH is so deadly that liberal scanning is justified.
  • Anticoagulation may not change ICH rate but increases mortality if intracranial hemorrhage occurs.
  • ED physical therapy is a powerful holistic intervention that reduces future fall-related visits.

Pitfalls

  • Using the term mechanical fall anchors to external cause and misses underlying fall syndrome.
  • Assuming anticoagulation is the main ICH risk factor; age itself is the biggest risk factor.
  • Skipping head CT because patient looks well with GCS 15; warfarin patients still have up to 9% ICH rate.
  • Assuming altered mental status is baseline without calling family or facility to verify.
