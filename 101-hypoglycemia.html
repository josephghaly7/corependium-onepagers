Hypoglycemia

Endocrine · Week 44 · src: Hypoglycemia
Bottom line: Hypoglycemia <70 mg/dL: check glucose in all altered/seizing/focal patients, give D50, add octreotide for sulfonylureas.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Obtain point-of-care glucose on every patient with altered mental status, seizure, or focal neuro deficit.
↓
2Place on cardiac monitor and continuous pulse oximetry; secure IV or IO access.
↓
3Most hypoglycemia patients do not require endotracheal intubation.
↓
4For seizing or obtunded patients, avoid intubation until after glucose is administered; mental status should improve rapidly.
↓
5If necessary, oxygen via nasal cannula or non-rebreather to keep oxygen saturation >90%; suction as needed.
↓
6For obtunded or seizing patients, give IV/IO dextrose bolus (D50 50 mL or 1 mL/kg IV); IO if no IV.
↓
7If no IV/IO access immediately available, give glucagon 1 mg IM.
↓
8For awake, alert patients able to swallow, give oral glucose.
↓
9If on a sulfonylurea, add octreotide 50-100 mcg subcutaneous after dextrose bolus; do not delay glucose correction.
↓
10Perform fingerstick glucose checks every hour until glucose is maintained; repeat D50 as needed.

📝 Key Facts

  • Hypoglycemia is defined as blood glucose <70 mg/dL (<3.9 mmol/L).
  • Hypoglycemia can present as unconscious, seizing, or focal neurological findings mimicking stroke.
  • No specific glucose level defines hypoglycemia or predicts symptoms.
  • 50 mg/dL equals 2.8 mmol/L; 4 mmol/L equals 72 mg/dL; 3 mmol/L equals 54 mg/dL.
  • One amp D50 (50 mL of 50% dextrose) is equivalent to about half a Snickers bar.
  • Glucagon 1 mg IM relies on hepatic stores; may fail in alcoholics, liver disease, or cancer, and can cause vomiting.
  • Sulfonylureas cause continued insulin release, leading to recurrent hypoglycemia despite initial D50.
  • Hypoglycemia is usually due to medication in all populations except neonates; bariatric surgery is an increasingly recognized cause even without diabetes.

Attending Pearls

  • Check it, check it on everybody; hypoglycemia can present as unconscious, seizing, or focal deficit.
  • Don't be afraid to give D50; it's just sugar.
  • If critical and no IV, go straight to IO rather than wasting time with glucagon.
  • Fix the glucose problem first before worrying about other management.
  • Do not delay glucose correction to give octreotide.

Pitfalls

  • Failing to check glucose in awake patients with focal neuro deficits; hypoglycemia can mimic stroke.
  • Assuming a specific glucose number predicts symptoms; no level defines hypoglycemia.
  • Relying on IM glucagon in patients with depleted hepatic stores, such as alcoholics or those with liver disease.
  • Treating sulfonylurea-induced hypoglycemia with glucose alone without octreotide, allowing recurrent bottoming out.
  • Intubating seizing or obtunded patients before administering glucose; mental status should improve rapidly after glucose.
