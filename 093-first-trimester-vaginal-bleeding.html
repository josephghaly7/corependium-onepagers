First Trimester Vaginal Bleeding

OB/GYN · Week 42 · src: Vaginal Bleeding in the Non-pregnant Patient
Bottom line: First-trimester bleeding: stabilize, pelvic exam, saline bolus, 2 units O-negative; conjugated estrogen 25 mg IV if needed.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess hemodynamic stability and recognize hypotension/tachycardia as hemorrhagic shock.
↓
2Ask bleeding onset/duration, pain, last menstrual period, pregnancy history, meds.
↓
3Have OB exam kit at bedside and start pelvic exam to find bleeding source while IV access obtained.
↓
4Rule out pregnancy with beta-hCG; obtain CBC.
↓
5Treat hypotension/shock with IV fluids and blood products.
↓
6Give saline bolus once IV line is in; transfuse 2 units O-negative if type-specific unavailable.
↓
7Consider massive transfusion protocol.
↓
8Reverse bleeding disorders if present or on anticoagulation.
↓
9Give conjugated estrogen 25 mg IV every 4-6 h until bleeding stops, max 24 h.
↓
10Place uterine tamponade device or vaginal packing; consult gynecology and IR immediately.

📝 Key Facts

  • Abnormal uterine bleeding affects up to 14% of women during reproductive years.
  • Critical ED tests: beta-hCG for pregnancy status and CBC.
  • Pelvic exam rules out structural/traumatic causes and quantifies bleeding.
  • Pelvic ultrasound is imaging modality of choice but can usually be deferred to outpatient setting.
  • Structural causes of AUB: fibroids, adenomyosis, polyps; non-structural: ovulatory dysfunction, coagulopathy, malignancy.
  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) are first-line to decrease bleeding severity and cramping.
  • All patients should be referred for timely gynecology follow-up, especially over 45 years to rule out endometrial cancer.
  • Dramatized unstable case: 30-year-old G1P1, 8 weeks gestation, BP 60s, HR 140.

Attending Pearls

  • You need a really solid approach to these patients because if you screw it up, it can be bad.
  • If she's unstable, stop the bleeding fast: pelvic exam, IV, saline bolus, O-negative blood.
  • Reassure the patient and family while resuscitating.
  • Hormonal medications (eg, estrogen) effectively temporize bleeding.
  • Consult gynecology and interventional radiology immediately in critical bleeding.

Pitfalls

  • Being unsure of the approach to unstable first-trimester bleeding.
  • Failing to ask about pregnancy history and bleeding disorders.
  • Not having an OB exam kit ready at the bedside.
  • Delaying saline bolus and O-negative blood in hemorrhagic shock.
  • Forgetting that pelvic ultrasound can usually be deferred to outpatient setting.
