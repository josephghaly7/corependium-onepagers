Approach to the Undifferentiated Poisoned Patient

Toxicology · Week 37 · src: Approach to the Undifferentiated Poisoned Patient
Bottom line: Structured ABCs approach with supportive care; ECG, glucose, decontamination, poison control are key.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Use PPE; decontaminate organophosphorus/carbamate/caustic exposures before patient contact with water or 0.9% sodium chloride.
↓
2Place on cardiac monitor with continuous pulse oximetry; secure IV access; obtain point-of-care glucose and treat hypoglycemia.
↓
3Clear airway of vomitus; consider reversible hypoglycemia and opioid toxicity before intubation.
↓
4Establish early definitive airway for loss/risk of airway protection, respiratory depression, caustic/thermal injury, angioedema or facial trauma.
↓
5If opioid toxidrome present, give naloxone immediately; use bag-valve-mask for decreased respiration or before RSI.
↓
6Obtain ECG; for QRS widening give sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 1-2 mEq/kg IV push, repeat if needed.
↓
7Treat hypotension with isotonic crystalloid 20 mL/kg IV; if refractory, give direct-acting vasopressor (eg, norepinephrine), avoid dopamine.
↓
8If pH <7.1 consider empiric treatment for cyanide, toxic alcohol, salicylate, CO, or metformin-associated lactic acidosis; hemodialysis if stable.
↓
9Consider activated charcoal or whole-bowel irrigation if not at risk for aspiration; fully expose to find persistent absorption sources.
↓
10Treat hyperthermia with aggressive cooling and lorazepam 1-2 mg IV push; consult toxicologist and/or poison center.
↓
11Check core temperature; consider RSI with rocuronium to reduce muscle activity in hyperthermia.

💊 Doses

Isotonic crystalloid20 mL/kg IV bolusFor hypotension/shock
Norepinephrine(dose not specified)Direct-acting vasopressor if hypotension refractory to IV fluids
Lorazepam1-2 mg IV pushFor hyperthermia
Naloxone(dose not specified)If opioid toxidrome present; administer immediately

📝 Key Facts

  • QRS widening suggests sodium channel blockade (eg, cyclic antidepressant) poisoning.
  • Naloxone should be given immediately if opioid toxidrome present.
  • Rocuronium is the recommended non-depolarizing paralytic for RSI in undifferentiated poisoned patients.
  • Post-intubation tidal volume 6-8 mL/kg recommended; match minute ventilation especially if tachypneic (eg, salicylate poisoning).
  • Isotonic crystalloid bolus 20 mL/kg IV for hypotension; be judicious in cardiogenic shock (calcium channel/beta blocker toxicity).
  • Avoid dopamine in poisonings as it is not a direct-acting vasopressor.
  • Severe acidosis defined as pH <7.1; sodium bicarbonate temporizing but not substitute for hemodialysis.
  • Empiric coma cocktail (D50, oxygen, naloxone, thiamine) no longer recommended.

Attending Pearls

  • The immediate approach to the poisoned patient is directed supportive care; there are only a few specific antidotes.
  • Rapid recognition of a toxidrome can suggest a class of toxin and direct immediate therapies.
  • An ECG and electrolyte analysis should be obtained as soon as possible, as these may direct therapy.
  • Poisonings following intentional self-harm require psychiatric evaluation and consultation.

Pitfalls

  • Delaying treatment or relying solely on laboratory analysis.
  • Giving activated charcoal or whole-bowel irrigation to patients at risk for aspiration (altered mental status, risk of seizure).
  • Using dopamine as a vasopressor in poisoning.
  • Being non-judicious with IV fluids in cardiogenic shock (calcium channel or beta blocker toxicity).
  • Using an empiric coma cocktail; it is no longer recommended.
