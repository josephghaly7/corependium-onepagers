Approach to the Undifferentiated Poisoned Patient

Toxicology · Week 37 · 9 min
Bottom line: Approach undifferentiated poisoned patient: ABCs, monitor, glucose, decontamination, poison control.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Start with ABCs; protect airway, support breathing, assess circulation and perfusion.
↓
2Place patient on cardiac monitor and obtain ECG to detect unstable arrhythmias.
↓
3Check point-of-care glucose in any patient with altered mental status.
↓
4Assess disability and consider trauma as alternative or coexisting cause.
↓
5Perform decontamination: GI only limited roles; don't forget skin and ocular.
↓
6Do full head-to-toe exposure exam, looking for hidden patches or toxins.
↓
7Call poison control or toxicologist; discuss plan and arrange longitudinal follow-up.
↓
8Assume intentional self-harm until proven otherwise; address safety and disposition.
↓
9If admitting, communicate need to revisit intubation and ongoing tox issues.
↓
10Reassess ABCs and response to interventions; use poison center for follow-up.

📝 Key Facts

  • Toxicology is often a diagnosis of exclusion.
  • Poison centers are staffed by specially trained nurses or pharmacists with physician backup.
  • Poison centers have provided telemedicine since the 1950s.
  • Calling poison control is a critical action on standardized exams.
  • Poison centers longitudinally follow patients until discharge or over acute/chronic toxicity.
  • D in tox approach includes dextrose, decontamination, disability; E is exposure.

Attending Pearls

  • Poison centers are the original telemedicine, available since the 1950s.
  • Poison control is the least likely consult to be snippy in the middle of the night.
  • Even straightforward ingestions warrant a poison center call to maintain funding and catch subtleties.
  • Poison center can reassure parents and continue follow-up after discharge.

Pitfalls

  • Skipping point-of-care glucose in altered mental status.
  • Forgetting skin and ocular decontamination, focusing only on GI.
  • Missing hidden toxin sources like fentanyl patches during head-to-toe exam.
  • Failing to call poison control; omission is a critical action on exams.
