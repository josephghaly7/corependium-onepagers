Global Initiative for Asthma Pediatric Guidelines

Pediatrics · Week 23 · src: Pediatric Asthma
Bottom line: GINA pediatric asthma: limit SABA, pair with ICS, IV MgSO4 25-50 mg/kg over 20 min for severe.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Avoid intubation; maximize oxygen, inhaled beta-agonist/anticholinergic, and systemic corticosteroids first.
↓
2For severe asthma with minimal aeration, give IM epinephrine while preparing inhaled therapy.
↓
3Nebulized albuterol 0.15-0.3 mg/kg (min 2.5 mg, max 5 mg) q20min x3, then q1-4h PRN; continuous 10-15 mg/hr if needed.
↓
4Add ipratropium with albuterol q20min x3: <20 kg 0.25 mg neb or 4 puffs MDI; >=20 kg 0.5 mg neb or 8 puffs MDI.
↓
5Give systemic corticosteroids ASAP: dexamethasone 0.3-0.6 mg/kg IV (max 16 mg) or methylprednisolone 1-2 mg/kg IV (max 125 mg).
↓
6If no improvement, give magnesium sulfate 25-50 mg/kg IV over 20 min (max 2000 mg).
↓
7Consider epinephrine infusion 0.01-0.5 mcg/kg/min titrated q5min, or terbutaline SC/infusion for severe exacerbation.
↓
8Resuscitate with 20 mL/kg normal saline or Ringer lactate for volume depletion; repeat to goal shock index >1 (1-3 y) or >1.2 (4-6 y).
↓
9For tiring patients, apply NIPPV with PEEP 5-8 cm H2O and IPAP 10-18 cm H2O unless altered mental status.
↓
10If intubating, use ketamine 2 mg/kg IV and rocuronium 1.5 mg/kg IV; maintain I:E 1:3-1:5, permissive hypercapnia pH >7.2.

📝 Key Facts

  • Target oxygen saturation 94-98%.
  • IM epinephrine dose: <30 kg give 0.01 mg/kg up to 0.3 mg; >=30 kg give 0.3 mg; >=50 kg give 0.5 mg; may repeat q5-15 min up to 3 doses.
  • Albuterol MDI with spacer: <20 kg give 4 inhalations q20min x3; >=20 kg give 8 inhalations q20min x3.
  • Ipratropium has slower onset (approx 30 min) and should be given with a beta2-agonist and corticosteroids.
  • Magnesium sulfate may cause hypotension; treat with 20 mL/kg isotonic fluid bolus.
  • High-flow nasal cannula flow: 2 L/kg for first 10 kg and 0.5 L/kg thereafter.
  • Signs of severe pediatric exacerbation: inability to speak in phrases, tripod positioning, altered mental status, tachypnea, accessory muscle use, hypoxia, peak flow <40-50% predicted/best, poor air movement, silent chest.
  • Asthma is more common and has higher mortality in Black children than White children.

Attending Pearls

  • Avoid endotracheal intubation of patients with asthma, when possible.
  • Sick patients with asthma tend to be volume depleted; resuscitate with isotonic fluids.
  • Use a nasal cannula or high-flow nasal cannula as needed (2 L/kg flow for first 10 kg and 0.5 L/kg thereafter).
  • Allow permissive hypercapnia, maintaining serum pH >7.2.
  • Magnesium is likely to confer a modest benefit that is greatest in critically ill patients.

Pitfalls

  • Intubating asthma patients without maximizing therapeutics; avoid endotracheal intubation when possible to prevent peri-intubation arrest.
  • Using non-invasive positive pressure ventilation in patients with altered mental status.
  • Missing anaphylaxis, which can mimic asthma exacerbation.
  • Giving ipratropium without a beta2-agonist or corticosteroids; it has slower onset and should be combined.
  • Ignoring hypotension from magnesium sulfate; treat with 20 mL/kg isotonic fluid bolus.
