Neonatal Rashes

Dermatology · Week 50 · 24 min
Bottom line: Most scary neonatal rashes are benign; identify HSV, syphilis, airway hemangioma.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess infant: febrile, ill-appearing, or impaired perfusion? If yes, pursue sepsis/HSV workup.
↓
2Look for vesicles, especially hidden; if HSV suspected, start IV acyclovir and admit.
↓
3If rash involves palms/soles in neonate, consider congenital syphilis; treat with penicillin and ID consult.
↓
4If hemangioma in beard distribution or midline, evaluate airway and refer urgently.
↓
5If hemangioma occludes vision, refer urgently to prevent lifelong vision implications.
↓
6If well-appearing with erythema toxicum or transient neonatal pustulosis, reassure; spares palms/soles, resolves.
↓
7If harlequin color change or milia/miliaria, reassure; keep dry, avoid occlusive creams.
↓
8If eczema/cradle cap, moisturize copiously; low-potency hydrocortisone for inflammation; mineral oil/brush for cradle cap.
↓
9If diaper rash: fungal creases -> antifungal; convex surfaces -> zinc barrier; refractory -> consider zinc/histiocytosis.
↓
10If scabies suspected, treat with 5% permethrin neck to toes overnight; repeat in one week.

💊 Doses

Permethrin 5%Topical: apply neck to toes, leave overnight, wash off; repeat in 1 weekNeonatal scabies

📝 Key Facts

  • Erythema toxicum appears within first 4 days, lasts about 4 days, spares palms/soles; baby well-appearing.
  • Transient neonatal pustulosis starts day 1, pustules without erythema; may leave pigmented macules with fine scale for weeks.
  • Harlequin color change lasts minutes, occurs first 3 weeks, more common in preemies.
  • Eczema usually appears at 2-3 months; low-potency OTC hydrocortisone plus frequent moisturizing.
  • HSV: early skin-eye-mouth acyclovir gives ~100% neurologically intact survival; CNS/systemic survival plummets; consider in neonatal seizure.
  • Palms/soles rash in neonate suggests congenital syphilis; treat with penicillin.

Attending Pearls

  • The worse the rash looks, the better it is—unless the child looks awful (febrile/poor perfusion).
  • Parents care more than baby; most benign neonatal rashes need reassurance and no treatment.
  • Eczema kids should be 'little grease pigs'—copious moisturizer to prevent flares.
  • If a 2-month-old is diagnosed with croup, check for airway hemangioma.

Pitfalls

  • Reassuring based on rash appearance alone; if febrile or impaired perfusion, treat as serious.
  • Missing HSV because vesicles are hidden or absent; consider acyclovir in neonatal seizure.
  • Diagnosing croup in a 2-month-old without considering airway hemangioma.
  • Scrubbing off all barrier cream with each diaper change worsens diaper rash; leave a film and reapply.
