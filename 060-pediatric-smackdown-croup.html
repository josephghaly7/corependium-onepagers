Pediatric Smackdown: Croup

Pediatrics · Week 23 · src: Croup
Bottom line: Croup: steroids for all (dexamethasone 0.15-0.6 mg/kg); nebulized epinephrine 0.5 mL/kg (max 5 mL) for stridor.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess severity: barky cough alone versus audible stridor and respiratory distress.
↓
2Give systemic dexamethasone 0.3 mg/kg orally to every child with croup.
↓
3If only barky cough, may discharge after oral steroid dose.
↓
4If audible stridor or respiratory distress, give nebulized epinephrine over about 15 minutes.
↓
5Racemic epinephrine 2.25% at 0.05-0.1 mL/kg (max 0.5 mL) diluted to 2-3 mL with 0.9% sodium chloride, every 15-20 minutes as needed.
↓
6Alternatively L-epinephrine 0.5 mL/kg (max 5 mL) of 1 mg/mL nebulized.
↓
7Confirm stridor resolves and child's comfort returns.
↓
8Observe 2 hours after epinephrine while steroids take effect (4-6 hours).
↓
9If improving, discharge with anticipatory guidance that barky cough may persist days.
↓
10For refractory severe croup, consider nebulized dexamethasone 0.3 mg/kg (max 8 mg) before intubation.

📝 Key Facts

  • Croup is the most common infectious cause of upper airway obstruction in children; accounts for >90% of stridor in children.
  • Most commonly occurs in children 6 months to 3 years old, but can occur in young infants and school-aged children.
  • Parainfluenza is the most common cause; antibiotics are overused and not indicated unless bacterial infection suspected.
  • In children with stridor at rest, racemic epinephrine 2.25% at 0.05-0.1 mL/kg nebulized (max 0.5 mL), diluted to 2-3 mL with 0.9% sodium chloride.
  • Dexamethasone 0.15-0.6 mg/kg PO/IV/IM one-time, maximum single dose 16 mg; IV formulation 4 mg/mL is more concentrated than liquid 1 mg/mL.
  • Nebulized budesonide is non-inferior to dexamethasone in children who cannot tolerate oral intake or have no IV access.
  • Systemic dexamethasone at 0.6, 0.3, or 0.15 mg/kg prevents return visits, progression, and hospitalization.
  • Nebulized dexamethasone alone is inferior to systemic for preventing repeat visits and hospital admissions.

Attending Pearls

  • If you have racemic epinephrine in your PIXIS, use it; if not, grab epinephrine off the code cart and nebulize 0.5 mL/kg.
  • The nice thing about nebulizing dexamethasone is it's a vasoconstrictor—takes effect quicker than 6 hours and mimics epinephrine's vasoconstriction.
  • Systemic steroids alone have such amazing outcomes that detecting added benefit from nebulized steroids would require hundreds of thousands of patients.
  • Only one study looked at adding nebulized steroid to oral steroid (budesonide) and showed no additional benefit; no study of nebulized dexamethasone on top of oral.
  • Suspect laryngomalacia when noisy breathing starts shortly after birth; symptoms peak at 6-8 months and resolve within 12-24 months.

Pitfalls

  • Using nebulized dexamethasone alone instead of systemic steroids—inferior for preventing return visits and hospital admissions.
  • Expecting dexamethasone to resolve the barky cough; it persists for days and steroids only prevent progression.
  • Sending home a stridulous patient immediately after epinephrine without 2-hour observation for steroid effect.
  • Giving nebulized dexamethasone to every mild croup patient lacks evidence and may prolong stay and agitate the child.
  • Assuming mental status changes are from croup—consider hypoglycemia, hypoxemia, severe dehydration, and electrolyte abnormalities.
