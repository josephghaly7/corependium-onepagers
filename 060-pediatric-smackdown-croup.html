Pediatric Smackdown: Croup

Pediatrics · Week 23 · 13 min
Bottom line: Croup: systemic dexamethasone for all; nebulized epinephrine for stridor; nebulized dexamethasone debated.

⚖ Management Pathway

● assess/decide   ● act/treat   ● refer/transfer
1Assess croup severity: barky cough alone vs audible stridor with respiratory distress.
↓
2Give systemic dexamethasone 0.3 mg/kg PO to every croup patient; 0.15 mg/kg may suffice.
↓
3If mild barky cough only, discharge after oral steroid; barky cough may persist days.
↓
4If audible stridor or respiratory distress, give nebulized epinephrine as temporizing measure.
↓
5Use racemic epinephrine if available; L-epinephrine 0.5 mL/kg (max 5 mL) of 1 mg/mL is equivalent.
↓
6Observe until stridor resolves and child is comfortable.
↓
7Watch 2 hours after epinephrine, by which time steroids have taken effect.
↓
8If doing well, discharge with anticipatory guidance that barky cough persists.
↓
9Consider nebulized dexamethasone 0.3 mg/kg (max 8 mg) for symptomatic stridor; evidence limited.
↓
10For refractory severe croup, nebulized dexamethasone may be tried before intubation.

💊 Doses

Dexamethasone0.3 mg/kg PO (0.15 mg/kg may suffice)Systemic steroid for all croup patients
Dexamethasone0.3 mg/kg nebulized (max 8 mg)IV solution nebulized for symptomatic croup; Al's approach
Racemic epinephrine2.25% nebulizedTemporizing measure for stridor/respiratory distress
L-epinephrine0.5 mL/kg (max 5 mL) of 1 mg/mL nebulizedAlternative to racemic epinephrine for severe croup

📝 Key Facts

  • Oral dexamethasone takes 4-6 hours to work; IV is no quicker.
  • Systemic dexamethasone at 0.6, 0.3, or 0.15 mg/kg prevents return visits, progression, and hospitalization.
  • Nebulized dexamethasone alone is inferior to systemic dexamethasone for preventing repeat visits and admissions.
  • Racemic and L-epinephrine have equal efficacy; L-epinephrine 0.5 mL/kg (max 5 mL) of 1 mg/mL is an alternative.
  • Barky cough persists for days despite steroids; steroids prevent progression, not cough resolution.
  • One study adding nebulized budesonide to oral steroid showed no additional benefit; no study of nebulized dexamethasone on top of oral.

Attending Pearls

  • Systemic dexamethasone is the mainstay; epinephrine is a temporizing bridge while steroids take effect.
  • Racemic and L-epinephrine are equally effective; use whichever is available in your department.
  • Nebulized dexamethasone may improve stridor and is worth trying in refractory severe croup before intubation.
  • Oral steroids alone have excellent outcomes, so detecting added benefit from nebulized steroids would require huge trials.

Pitfalls

  • Using nebulized dexamethasone alone instead of systemic steroids—it is inferior for preventing return visits and admissions.
  • Expecting dexamethasone to resolve the barky cough; it persists for days and steroids only prevent progression.
  • Sending home a stridulous patient immediately after epinephrine without 2-hour observation for steroid effect.
  • Giving nebulized dexamethasone to every mild croup patient lacks evidence and may prolong stay and agitate the child.
